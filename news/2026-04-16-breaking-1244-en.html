Sweden Lowers Criminal Age to 13 in Historic Youth Justice Overhaul

Latest news and analysis from Sweden's Riksdag. AI-generated political intelligence based on OSINT/INTOP data covering parliament, government, and agencies with systematic transparency.
16 April 2026 12:44 UTC • 🔴 Breaking • 6 min read • 🔴 HIGH • 🔴 HIGH RISK

Prime Minister Ulf Kristersson and Justice Minister Gunnar Strömmer today tabled Sweden's most sweeping youth criminal justice reform in 124 years. Proposition 2025/26:246 proposes lowering the age of criminal responsibility from 15 to 13 for serious crimes, abolishing sentencing reductions for 18-20 year olds entirely, and raising the maximum prison term for offenders under 18 to 18 years — a fundamental break with Sweden's historically rehabilitation-centered juvenile justice philosophy.

What Is Happening

The Swedish government has submitted Proposition 2025/26:246 "Skärpta regler för unga lagöverträdare" (Stricter Rules for Young Offenders) to the Riksdag on April 16, 2026. The bill, signed by PM Kristersson and Justice Minister Strömmer from Justitiedepartementet, proposes five major reforms effective August 2, 2026:

  1. Criminal responsibility at age 13: For serious crimes, the age of criminal responsibility drops from 15 to 13 — a temporary measure with a five-year sunset clause. Sweden has maintained the age-15 threshold since 1902.
  2. Youth discount abolished for 18-20 year olds: The so-called ungdomsreduktion — which reduces sentences for young adults — is completely eliminated for those aged 18-20.
  3. Reduced youth discount for under-18s: For offenders under 18, the sentencing discount is narrowed and the maximum sentence raised to 18 years imprisonment (previously significantly lower).
  4. Tighter youth supervision: The ungdomsövervakning regime is made substantially stricter.
  5. Stricter breach consequences: Penalties for violating terms of ungdomsvård and ungdomstjänst are toughened.

The proposition requires 31 separate law amendments across the criminal code (brottsbalken), social insurance code, and multiple specialized statutes. It has been referred to the Committee on Justice (JuU).

Why It Matters

This proposition represents a paradigm shift in Swedish criminal justice philosophy. For over a century, Sweden has been a global exemplar of rehabilitation-focused juvenile justice. The decision to lower the criminal age to 13 — making Sweden an outlier even within the Nordic region (Denmark, Norway, and Finland all maintain age 15) — signals how profoundly youth gang violence has reshaped the political landscape.

The reform arrives in the context of an escalating government offensive on organized crime. In the past two weeks alone, the Kristersson government has tabled:

  • Prop. 2025/26:218: Double penalties for crimes committed in criminal networks
  • Prop. 2025/26:217: Extended criminal liability for civil servants
  • A new indefinite sentence for serious repeat offenders (effective April 15)
  • Investigation into blocking social benefits for convicted criminals
  • New seizure rules targeting criminal economies

Political Landscape

The proposition is tabled on a day of intense parliamentary activity on criminal justice. The Riksdag is currently debating Kriminalvårdsfrågor (Correctional Services Issues) with speakers from all eight parties — including V's Gudrun Nordborg, M's Mikael Damsgaard, SD's Pontus Andersson Garpvall, S's Anna Wallentheim, KD's Ingemar Kihlström, C's Ulrika Liljeberg, and MP's Ulrika Westerlund.

The government held a press conference at 13:00 dedicated to the proposition. PM's Question Time is scheduled for 14:00 and voting for 15:20 — both likely to feature youth crime prominently.

Winners and Losers

Winners

  • Kristersson government (M+KD+L): Delivers on core campaign promise of tougher youth crime laws before 2026 election. Justice Minister Strömmer cements his legacy as the architect of Sweden's most comprehensive criminal justice overhaul in a generation.
  • SD: Sees its long-standing demand for stricter youth crime rules implemented by the government it supports. Strengthens narrative that SD influence produces concrete policy results.
  • Law enforcement: Gains new tools to prosecute young gang members who currently evade criminal responsibility.

Losers

  • Children's rights advocates: Barnombudsmannen and organizations like Rädda Barnen face a direct challenge to the UN Convention on the Rights of the Child, which Sweden incorporated into domestic law in 2020.
  • V and MP: Their opposition to lowering the criminal age will be framed as being "soft on crime" in the pre-election narrative.
  • S: Caught in an uncomfortable position — supporting the principle of tougher measures while potentially opposing the specific age-13 threshold. Any nuanced position risks being simplified in media coverage.
  • SiS (Statens institutionsstyrelse): Must accommodate 13-14 year olds in secure facilities with no clear capacity plan.
