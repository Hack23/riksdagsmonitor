Les Trois Propositions

Prop 2025/26:267 : Protection renforcée contre les menaces sécuritaires qualifiées [HD03267]

Le texte le plus sensible sur le plan constitutionnel étend les outils juridiques suédois pour expulser les étrangers que le SÄPO considère comme des « menaces sécuritaires qualifiées ». La saisine du Lagrådet est quasi certaine compte tenu de son impact sur les droits fondamentaux (CEDH Arts. 3, 8, 13).

Arithmétique parlementaire : La coalition Tidö détient 181 sièges (majorité requise : 175). Adoption quasi certaine (Probabilité : 90%).

Prop 2025/26:261 : Pouvoirs étendus pour le Skatteverket [HD03261]

Donne au Skatteverket des pouvoirs accrus pour lutter contre les fraudes à l'enregistrement de population. Statskontoret estime environ 50 000 faux enregistrements et 2 milliards de SEK de coûts annuels.

Prop 2025/26:250 : Un e-ID étatique [HD03250]

Crée un cadre légal pour une identité numérique gratuite émise par l'État. Environ 1 million de Suédois n'ont pas accès à BankID.

Contexte électoral

La Suède vote le 13 septembre 2026. La coalition Tidö est à environ 47-49% dans les sondages.

Tidö Government Launches Pre-Election Legislative Push: Security, Surveillance, and Digital Identity

Three government propositions submitted 7 May 2026 advance the ruling coalition's final legislative programme before Sweden's September 2026 general election. The package spans digital infrastructure, population-registry integrity, and national security — and draws its sharpest political battle lines around the deportation of qualified security threats.

Overview

The Swedish government submitted three propositions to the Riksdag on 7 May 2026, published 12 May 2026. The bundle forms a coherent pre-election policy statement from the Tidö coalition (Moderaterna, Sverigedemokraterna, Kristdemokraterna, Liberalerna), projecting state competence in three contested domains: digital inclusion, anti-fraud, and counter-terrorism.

With just four months until the 13 September 2026 general election, the legislative window is narrow. Each proposition has been referred to its respective parliamentary committee — Transport Committee (TU), Tax Committee (SkU), and Justice Committee (JuU) — and must complete its committee journey and plenary vote before the June parliamentary recess to enter into force as intended.

The Three Propositions

Prop 2025/26:267: Strengthened Protection Against Qualified Security Threats [HD03267]

Status: Referred to Justice Committee (JuU) | DIW: 82 (Highest)

The most constitutionally significant of the three, Prop 2025/26:267 expands Sweden's legal tools for expelling or denying entry to foreign nationals deemed "qualified security threats" by SÄPO, the Swedish Security Service. The proposition amends the Aliens Act (Utlänningslagen) to widen the definition of qualifying threats, enhance SÄPO's advisory role in migration proceedings, and accelerate the judicial review timeline for security deportation cases.

Why it matters: The proposition sits at the intersection of Sweden's hardest political tensions — national security and fundamental rights. The government argues that current procedures are too slow and cumbersome for a threat environment that includes Russian intelligence operations, Islamist networks, and organised crime with foreign connections.

Critics, including Vänsterpartiet, Miljöpartiet, and civil society groups such as Civil Rights Defenders, argue that the proposition creates a two-tier justice system where individuals can lose residence rights based on classified evidence they cannot see or challenge — a potential violation of ECHR Articles 6, 8, and 13, and of the EU Charter of Fundamental Rights Article 47.

Lagrådet referral virtually certain: Legal experts expect the proposition to be referred to Sweden's Law Council (Lagrådet) for constitutional review, given its direct impact on fundamental rights under Chapter 2 of the Regeringsformen. If Lagrådet identifies significant compatibility issues — particularly the lack of a "special advocate" system for reviewing classified evidence — the government will face a choice between revision and political cost.

Voting mathematics: With 181 seats, the Tidö coalition holds a majority over the 175-seat threshold. Passage is virtually certain (WEP: 90%). The key variables are Centerpartiet's reservations on rule-of-law grounds, and whether Socialdemokraterna's security wing supports the proposition or maintains opposition. An S support declaration would be politically transformative.

Prop 2025/26:261: Expanded Powers for Skatteverket in Population Registry Activities [HD03261]

Status: Referred to Tax Committee (SkU) | DIW: 68

Prop 2025/26:261 gives Sweden's Tax Agency (Skatteverket) enhanced authority to combat identity and address fraud in the national population registry (folkbokföringen). The proposition enables Skatteverket to initiate identity verification without individual complaints, mandates data-sharing with police and municipalities for fraud suspects, and introduces new penalties for repeat offenders.

The fraud problem: Statskontoret, Sweden's agency for public administration review, estimated in 2024 that approximately 50,000 individuals are registered at false addresses in Sweden's SPAR database, costing the state an estimated 2 billion SEK annually through fraudulent welfare payments, incorrect tax calculations, and false benefit entitlements. The Tidö coalition committed in its 2022 Tidöavtalet to significantly reduce folkbokföringsbrott.

Privacy stakes: The proposition's expansion of state data-processing powers is its most contested dimension. Sweden's data protection authority, IMY (Integritetsskyddsmyndigheten), is expected to assess compliance with GDPR Article 5 (purpose limitation) and Article 35 (mandatory DPIA for systematic large-scale processing). A negative IMY advisory opinion could force SkU committee amendments, though passage remains likely.

Cross-party dynamics: Historically, combating welfare fraud is a Socialdemokraterna issue. The Tidö government is deliberately occupying this policy ground, forcing S into a difficult choice: support the proposition (ceding political territory to the coalition) or oppose it (risking being labelled as soft on fraud). Vänsterpartiet will oppose on surveillance grounds.

Prop 2025/26:250: A State Digital Identity [HD03250]

Status: Referred to Transport Committee (TU) | DIW: 52

The broadest in scope but lowest in political contestation, Prop 2025/26:250 creates a statutory framework for a government-issued digital identity (statlig e-legitimation). A new state authority will issue e-ID credentials meeting the EU eIDAS 2.0 "High" assurance level, free of charge to all citizens and legal residents, and mandatory for acceptance by all public authorities.

The digital inclusion argument: Approximately one million Swedes — 10% of the population — currently lack access to BankID, Sweden's dominant private-sector digital identity issued by a banking consortium. They are disproportionately elderly, recently arrived immigrants, and low-income individuals. As government services migrate online, this gap creates systematic exclusion.

The BankID disruption: The proposition will face intensive lobbying from the BankID consortium (comprising Sweden's major banks) and competitors like Freja eID+ during TU committee hearings. The banking industry will seek "technology neutrality" language that preserves their market position alongside the state system. The Danish precedent — the transition from NemID (private banking) to MitID (state) completed 2021–2022 — provides a template, though the Swedish transition is designed as complementary rather than replacement.

EU alignment: Sweden is required to comply with EU eIDAS 2.0 (Regulation 2024/1183), which mandates that member states offer a national digital identity option by 2027. This European deadline provides additional impetus independent of electoral considerations.

The Pre-Election Political Context

Sweden votes on 13 September 2026 — approximately 124 days from the tabling of these propositions. Current polling places the Tidö coalition at approximately 47–49%, with projected seats in the range of 171–181 — a borderline majority. The opposition bloc (S + V + MP + C) is polling at approximately 44–46%.

This legislative package serves multiple pre-election functions simultaneously:

  1. Security credibility (HD03267): Confirms Tidö's law-and-order positioning ahead of a campaign in which migration and security remain the top concerns for SD's voter base — and a significant share of M's.

  2. Administrative competence (HD03261): Demonstrates "we fix problems" governing capacity against S's traditional ownership of welfare-system integrity.

  3. Digital Sweden (HD03250): Signals forward-looking governance to urban professional voters who lean L and M; satisfies EU compliance obligations.

Economic Context

IMF World Economic Outlook, April 2026 (vintage: WEO-2026-04, age: 1 month)

Sweden's economy is projected to grow by 2.4% in 2026, recovering from the near-stagnation of 2023–2024. The fiscal position shows a general government balance of approximately -0.8% of GDP, consistent with ongoing consolidation. The propositions carry marginal fiscal implications: HD03261's fraud savings (~2bn SEK/year) are the most significant; HD03250's implementation cost (~500m SEK over 5 years) is offset by long-term efficiency gains.
