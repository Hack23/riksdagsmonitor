Policy and Scrutiny: The Week Ahead in the Riksdag

The government moves to digitalize housing rights and simplify family benefits, while the opposition directs intense scrutiny at the Prime Minister's inner circle.

STOCKHOLM – In a week marked by administrative modernization and sharpening political conflict, the Swedish government has put forward two significant propositions aimed at streamlining bureaucracy. Meanwhile, the political temperature is rising over the conduct of high-ranking officials.

A Registry for Every Home

The government has submitted Proposition 2025/26:112, "Ett register för alla bostadsrätter" (A register for all tenant-ownership rights). This proposal aims to create a centralized, digital register for bostadsrätter, replacing the current fragmented system kept by individual housing associations.

Ideally, this will increase transparency in the housing market, make mortgaging easier, and protect consumer rights. It represents a significant step towards the full digitalization of Swedish property records, aligning with broader EU trends for financial transparency.

Simplifying Parenthood

In a move to reduce administrative friction for families, the government also presented Proposition 2025/26:117. This bill proposes abolishing the requirement to notify the Social Insurance Agency (Försäkringskassan) before applying for parental benefit. This small but significant change is designed to make the system more flexible and less punitive for parents who might miss strict deadlines.

  • Prop. 2025/26:117 - Parental Leave

Security Advisor Under Fire

While the government focuses on policy, the opposition is focusing on accountability. Social Democrat MP Laila Naraghi has submitted a series of written questions (HD11481–HD11484) to Prime Minister Ulf Kristersson and Justice Minister Gunnar Strömmer regarding National Security Advisor Henrik Landerholm.

The questions concern a reported police report and whether proper public employment laws were followed in the Government Offices. This line of questioning suggests the opposition sees a vulnerability in the administration's handling of national security appointments, a sensitive topic given the current geopolitical climate.

Baltic Security Concerns

Adding to the security theme, Sweden Democrats' Markus Wiechel has raised the issue of underwater cable sabotage in the Baltic Sea (Question HD11486), framing it as part of hybrid warfare threats. This aligns with increasing concerns about critical infrastructure protection in the region.

Analysis

The government is proceeding with its structural reform agenda, focusing on digitalization and deregulation. However, the questions surrounding the National Security Advisor indicate that the opposition intends to keep the government on the defensive regarding governance and integrity issues. As the week progresses, expect these written questions to evolve into sharper debates in the Chamber.
