Week Ahead: Security Advisor Scandal & Digital Defense

February 12, 2026 Riksdagsmonitor Intelligence

STOCKHOLM — The political week is dominated by intensifying scrutiny over the Prime Minister's handling of the National Security Advisor controversy, while concerns over Baltic Sea infrastructure security prompt new demands for defense capabilities. Opposition parties are leveraging parliamentary questions to press the government on accountability and national resilience.

The Security Advisor Controversy

The Social Democrats (S) have launched a coordinated series of questions targeting Prime Minister Ulf Kristersson regarding the National Security Advisor, Henrik Landerholm. MP Laila Naraghi has submitted multiple formal questions (HD11484, HD11483) concerning the police report filed against the advisor and the applicability of public employment laws to the Government Offices. The opposition is probing whether the advisor's close personal ties to the Prime Minister have influenced the handling of alleged misconduct, signaling a potential constitutional committee review.

Baltic Sea Security

Defense issues remain at the forefront as the Sweden Democrats (SD) raise alarms about hybrid warfare in the Baltic Sea. MP Markus Wiechel has questioned Defense Minister Pål Jonson about the sabotage of underwater cables (HD11486), citing recent incidents as evidence of escalating threats, primarily from Russia. This aligns with a broader push for strengthened naval presence and surveillance capabilities to protect critical infrastructure.

Legislative Agenda

Beyond the headline controversies, the parliamentary committees are moving forward with significant legislative work:

  • Free Speech & Defamation: A recent court verdict against the publisher of "Dumpen" has triggered a debate on defamation laws, with SD questioning Justice Minister Gunnar Strömmer on whether current legislation adequately balances free speech with privacy protections (HD11488).
  • Committee Reports: The Social Insurance Committee (SfU) is proposing to abolish the requirement for prior notification before applying for parental benefits, a move aimed at simplifying bureaucracy for families (HD01SfU20). Meanwhile, the Environment and Agriculture Committee (MJU) is finalizing a report on animal welfare (HD01MJU9), which faces scrutiny from the Centre Party regarding rising veterinary costs (HD11487).

Analysis

The convergence of security policy and administrative accountability creates a dual-front challenge for the Kristersson government. While they maintain a strong posture on national defense—a stance generally supported across the aisle—the internal administrative questions regarding the Security Advisor provide the opposition with a tactical wedge issue. The Social Democrats are likely to keep this issue alive to question the government's judgment and adherence to administrative norms, potentially overshadowing the government's legislative wins in committee.
