SD Challenges Coalition Partners on Mosque Extremism and Assembly Rights

Latest news and analysis from Sweden's Riksdag. AI-generated political intelligence based on OSINT/INTOP data covering parliament, government, and agencies with systematic transparency.
8 April 2026 • Analysis • 8 min read • 🟡 MEDIUM • ⚠️ MODERATE RISK

Sverigedemokraterna has filed two politically charged interpellations targeting coalition ministers on religious extremism and constitutional rights. Richard Jomshof (SD) demands Social Minister Jakob Forssmed (KD) act on documented hate preaching in Kristianstad mosques, while Rashid Farivar (SD) challenges Justice Minister Gunnar Strömmer (M) over Proposition 2025/26:133, warning it could create a "heckler's veto" undermining demonstration freedom. These filings from the government's own support party signal growing coalition tensions as the 2026 election approaches.

Interpellation Debates

In a striking display of intra-coalition accountability, SD's two latest interpellations target the government's own KD and M ministers on ideologically sensitive issues — mosque-based religious extremism and the constitutional limits of security legislation. Combined with 13 other recent interpellations spanning transport infrastructure, integration policy, disability rights, and foreign affairs, these filings reveal a parliament where the government faces pressure from all sides, including its own support base.

Opposition Strategy

Interpellations from 3 different parties demonstrate coordinated parliamentary oversight and demands for government accountability.
