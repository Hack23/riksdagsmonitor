Deep Inspection: Sweden's Cybersecurity Offensive

8 March 2026 • Deep Inspection • 12 min read

Key Takeaways

  • 91 Measures Against Cyber Threats: On 5 March 2026, the government updated its national cybersecurity action plan from 72 to 91 activities, with 21 new measures targeting critical infrastructure, public administration, and digital supply chains
  • Cybersecurity Act (2025:1506): Sweden's new cybersecurity law, implementing the EU's NIS 2 directive, entered into force on 11 December 2025 with comprehensive requirements for incident reporting, risk management, and enforcement
  • SEK 250 Million for Municipalities: The 2026 budget allocates 250 million kronor specifically to increase municipal and regional resilience against cyber threats through NIS 2 implementation
  • EU Cybersecurity Package: The Riksdag is reviewing EU proposals (COM(2026) 11) to strengthen ENISA and update the Cybersecurity Act, with implications for Sweden's national framework

91 Measures to Strengthen Sweden's Cyber Resilience

Minister for Civil Defence Carl-Oskar Bohlin announced on 5 March 2026 that the government has updated Sweden's national cybersecurity action plan, expanding it from 72 to 91 activities. The updated plan, which concretizes the goals of the National Cybersecurity Strategy 2025–2029, introduces 21 entirely new measures while updating 13 existing ones to raise ambition levels or reflect new EU regulations.

The new activities focus on four key areas: strengthening the National Cybersecurity Center (NCSC) with additional resources; developing cybersecurity capabilities in public administration through national mapping exercises, safer procurement, and coordinated government IT operations; protecting critical infrastructure and digital supply chains through new guidelines and monitoring models; and investing in competence, research, education, and broader societal cybersecurity awareness.

"The work to strengthen our collective resilience against threats in the cyber domain continues with undiminished force," Bohlin stated. "By updating the national cybersecurity action plan, we ensure progress and momentum toward the goals the government has set."

The New Cybersecurity Act: Sweden Implements NIS 2

The Cybersecurity Act (2025:1506), which entered into force on 11 December 2025, represents the most significant overhaul of Swedish information security law in a decade. The Defence Committee's report (FöU2) on the government's proposition "A Strong Protection for Network and Information Systems" passed with broad parliamentary support.

The new law imposes comprehensive obligations on operators of essential and important services, including mandatory risk management measures, incident reporting within 24 hours of detection for significant incidents (with a detailed report due within 72 hours), and regular security audits. Enforcement mechanisms include administrative sanctions with fines of up to 2 percent of global annual turnover for essential entities.

The accompanying Cybersecurity Ordinance (2025:1507) details the specific requirements for sector-specific implementation, designates responsible supervisory authorities, and establishes the coordination framework between the Swedish Civil Contingencies Agency (MSB), the National Cybersecurity Center, and CERT-SE.

Parliamentary Debate

During the debate on FöU2, MPs from both government and opposition parties emphasized that cybersecurity is a shared responsibility spanning from individual citizens to the state. The opposition noted that an earlier National Audit Office review had found that cybersecurity work had failed under the previous government (2017–2022 review period) due to inadequate political steering, funding, and coordination. The current government's response has been a dramatic acceleration through the new law, increased funding, and the 91-measure action plan.

Cybersecurity as National Crisis Preparedness

The Defence Committee's report on societal crisis preparedness (FöU9, dated 25 February 2026) places cybersecurity firmly within the broader framework of civil defence and total defence. The report references the National Cybersecurity Strategy 2025–2029 (Skr. 2024/25:121) as a central element of crisis preparedness, replacing the earlier strategy from 2016/17.

The committee report details how the government's 2026 budget proposal includes dedicated funding for national cybersecurity, including the National Cybersecurity Center's work in developing Sweden's collective capability to prevent, detect, and manage antagonistic cyber threats and IT incidents. The report connects cybersecurity to Sweden's broader defence posture, noting that "cyber defence and cybersecurity are mutually reinforcing."

The National Audit Office's review of civil defence governance (FöU7) further underscores these concerns, finding that the National Cybersecurity Center had not lived up to expectations during the 2017–2022 period. The current government has responded with the expanded 91-measure plan and increased institutional capacity.

EU Cybersecurity Package: Next Steps

The Riksdag's EU fact sheet (FPM78, dated 23 February 2026) examines the European Commission's cybersecurity package (COM(2026) 11), which proposes amendments to both the EU Cybersecurity Act and the NIS 2 directive. Key proposals include:

  • Strengthened ENISA: The European cybersecurity agency would receive enhanced capacity for incident response coordination and a clearer role in developing the European Cybersecurity Skills Framework (ECSF)
  • Updated NIS 2: Modifications to the directive that would affect Sweden's newly enacted cybersecurity law, potentially requiring legislative amendments
  • Cyber Resilience Act: Complementary EU legislation (examined in SOU 2025:115) establishing cybersecurity requirements for products with digital elements placed on the EU market

The fact sheet notes that the previously existing National Liaison Officers network is not included in the new proposal, signaling a shift toward more centralized EU-level coordination. Sweden's government position on these proposals will be critical given the country's recent investment in its own national framework.

Budget and Resource Allocation

The 2026 budget allocates SEK 250 million specifically for NIS 2 implementation to increase municipal and regional resilience against cyber threats. This represents a significant investment in local-level cybersecurity capabilities, reflecting the recognition that municipalities and regions operate critical services in healthcare, education, and public safety that are increasingly vulnerable to cyberattacks.

During the budget debate on 17 December 2025 (Protocol 54), MPs from across the political spectrum acknowledged the necessity of this investment. The Finance Committee's report (FiU3) on general grants to municipalities highlighted the NIS 2 funding as part of broader efforts to strengthen security and quality in the welfare sector.

Additional funding flows through the government's broader defence and crisis preparedness budget (FöU1), which supports the National Cybersecurity Center, MSB's CERT-SE function, and cross-sector cybersecurity coordination.

  • Municipal Readiness: With 250 million kronor earmarked, the pace of NIS 2 implementation at the local level will be a key indicator of Sweden's actual cyber resilience
  • NCSC Development: The National Cybersecurity Center's enhanced mandate and resources should translate into measurable improvements in incident response capacity
