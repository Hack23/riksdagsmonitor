Finance Committee intensifies spring budget review while cross-party defence consultations shape Sweden's NATO commitments. Migration and energy policy motions queue for chamber debate as Riksdag enters crucial pre-Easter legislative sprint.

Why This Week Matters

The Riksdag enters a pivotal week that will shape the legislative agenda through Easter. Three defining threads run through the parliamentary calendar: the Finance Committee's spring budget review sets the fiscal parameters for the remainder of the session; cross-party defence consultations test whether Sweden's NATO commitments can sustain broad political support; and a cluster of committee reports on migration, energy, and criminal justice signal where the government's reform ambitions face their stiffest headwinds.

Monday: Budget Framework and Defence Briefings

The week opens with the Finance Committee's first formal review session on the spring budget framework — a procedural milestone that reveals the government's fiscal priorities before autumn's full budget presentation. Committee Chair Elisabeth Svantesson's opening remarks will be closely watched for signals on whether the Tido coalition maintains unity on tax reform and public spending.

Simultaneously, the Defence Committee receives its quarterly NATO capability assessment briefing. With Sweden's first full year of NATO membership now complete, the session will evaluate progress on the capability targets agreed in the bilateral defence cooperation agreements. The briefing comes against a backdrop of intensifying European defence debates and shifting transatlantic dynamics.

The Social Affairs Committee's hearing on healthcare staffing completes Monday's agenda, addressing the persistent nursing shortage that has become one of the most pressing domestic policy challenges across Swedish regions.

Tuesday: Energy Debate and Justice Review

Tuesday's chamber debate on the government's energy policy proposition represents a significant test of the coalition's ability to bridge internal tensions. The proposition, which outlines the framework for Sweden's energy mix through 2040, has drawn sharp criticism from both environmental advocates who demand faster renewable deployment and industry voices pushing for expanded nuclear capacity.

The energy proposition consolidates several earlier consultation rounds and attempts to chart a middle course between the Moderates' nuclear ambitions and the Liberals' emphasis on market-driven solutions. The debate will also feature questions from the Social Democrats and Green Party, whose alternative motion calls for a binding 2035 renewable energy target.

In parallel, the Justice Committee examines the latest package of organised crime measures, including enhanced tools for law enforcement digital surveillance and cross-border cooperation frameworks. The measures represent the government's continued prioritisation of security policy ahead of the next election.

Wednesday: EU Defence and Migration Reform

Wednesday brings two of the week's most consequential sessions. The EU Committee's briefing on European defence integration comes at a critical moment, with Brussels pushing for greater coordination on procurement and capability development. Sweden's position — balancing Nordic defence cooperation, NATO integration, and EU strategic autonomy — remains one of the most complex foreign policy puzzles facing the Kristersson government.

The Migration Committee's asylum system reform report, expected to be published Wednesday morning, could prove the week's most politically charged document. The report consolidates the committee's review of Sweden's asylum processing system and is expected to recommend significant structural changes to the Migration Agency's decision-making processes.

The afternoon's interpellation debate on housing policy, initiated by Centre Party leader Muharrem Demirok, will press Housing Minister Andreas Carlson on the government's response to the construction industry downturn and the persistent housing shortage in Sweden's major cities.

The Legislative Sprint

With Easter recess approaching in early April, the Riksdag faces a compressed legislative calendar. More than 40 committee reports are queued for debate and vote before the recess, covering everything from digital infrastructure investment to forestry regulation reform. The government's ability to maintain legislative momentum depends critically on the cooperation of the Sweden Democrats, whose confidence-and-supply arrangement underpins the coalition's parliamentary majority.

Thursday: Votes and Cross-Party Consultations

Thursday's voting session processes the accumulated committee reports and motions from the preceding weeks. While most votes are expected to follow predictable party lines, several reports on social policy may test the boundaries of the Tido Agreement's policy frameworks.

The day's most significant event may be the cross-party defence consultations in the afternoon. These structured discussions — a Swedish parliamentary tradition for building consensus on national security — bring together all eight parties to discuss long-term defence planning. With defence spending now approaching the NATO target of 2% of GDP, the consultations will address force structure, procurement timelines, and the division of responsibilities between national and allied capabilities.

Friday: Economic Forecast and Forward Look

The week closes with the Finance Committee's economic forecast hearing, featuring presentations from the National Institute of Economic Research (Konjunkturinstitutet) and the Riksbank. The hearing provides critical context for the spring budget deliberations, with particular attention to inflation trajectory, labour market conditions, and the fiscal space available for new policy commitments.

Committee reports scheduled for publication on Friday will set the agenda for the following week's chamber debates, maintaining the legislative momentum the government needs to deliver on its pre-Easter ambitions.
