Legislative Update

Government Moves on Security Detention and Housing Registry

In a dual push for tighter control over both physical security and financial assets, the Swedish government has advanced legislative proposals on indefinite security detention and a national housing registry, sparking debate on civil liberties and privacy.

Context

The administration of Prime Minister Ulf Kristersson continues to prioritize law and order reforms. Proposition 2025/26:112, "A Register for All Condominiums" (Ett register för alla bostadsrätter), aims to close loopholes used for money laundering and organized crime by establishing a centralized ownership database. Concurrently, the government is pushing Proposition 2025/26:95, "Security Detention" (Säkerhetsförvaring), introducing a new form of indefinite detention for dangerous offenders, a significant shift in Swedish penal philosophy.

Evidence

Documents retrieved from the Riksdag database reveal significant legislative activity. Proposition 2025/26:112 was formally submitted on February 5, 2026, by the Ministry of Justice. It argues that the current lack of a central registry hampers police investigations into illegal subletting and asset concealment.

Meanwhile, opposition parties have mobilized against the security detention proposal. Motions filed by the Centre Party (Motion 2025/26:3901) and the Green Party (Motion 2025/26:3902) on February 6 challenge the government's approach. The Centre Party's motion, led by Ulrika Liljeberg, calls for the rejection of the proposal, citing concerns over legal certainty and the potential for indefinite incarceration without new criminal acts. The Green Party motion proposes amendments to strictly limit the application of the law.

Analysis

The housing registry proposal is likely to pass with broad support, as it addresses a technical vulnerability in Sweden's property market that has long been exploited. However, the security detention measure represents a deeper ideological divide. By introducing indeterminate sentences based on perceived risk rather than past acts, the government is moving Sweden closer to models used in Germany and the UK. The opposition's pushback highlights the tension between public safety and individual rights, a defining theme of the current mandate.

Conclusion

As these bills move through committee, the debate will likely intensify. The housing registry offers a practical tool against organized crime, while the security detention law challenges the fundamental principles of the Swedish justice system. The outcome will signal how far the Riksdag is willing to go in its paradigm shift on crime and punishment.

Sources: Prop. 2025/26:112, Prop. 2025/26:95, Mot. 2025/26:3901, Mot. 2025/26:3902. Data retrieved via Riksdagsmonitor MCP.
