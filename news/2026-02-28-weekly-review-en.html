Weekly Review: A Decisive Week for Swedish Security and Social Policy

28 February 2026 • Weekly Review • 12 min read

Analysis of parliamentary activity 21–28 February 2026: 10 government propositions, 15 committee reports, and sweeping government action on cybersecurity, civil protection, and social insurance reform

The last week of February marked one of the most legislatively productive stretches of the 2025/26 parliamentary session. The government tabled ten propositions spanning national defence, criminal law, migration policy and social insurance — a breadth that underscores the coalition's push to advance its agenda before the spring recess. Meanwhile, 15 committee reports reached the chamber floor, and a wave of government press releases signalled new cybersecurity legislation and expanded Baltic Sea surveillance.

Week Summary: Top Five Developments

  1. Social insurance qualification reform (Prop. 2025/26:136): The government's landmark welfare-reform proposition, requiring immigrants to earn eligibility for Swedish social benefits, advanced to committee — the most politically charged proposal of the week.
  2. Civil protection overhaul (Prop. 2025/26:142): A new proposition from Defence Minister Carl-Oskar Bohlin strengthens civilian protection during heightened military preparedness, reflecting Sweden's post-NATO-accession security posture.
  3. Criminalisation of psychological violence (Prop. 2025/26:138): Justice Minister Gunnar Strömmer tabled a new criminal offence for psychological violence — a first in Swedish law.
  4. Cybersecurity legislation: The government announced new laws to strengthen Sweden's cybersecurity framework, alongside measures to build resilience in critical infrastructure.
  5. Energy policy committee marathon: The Industry Committee (NU) rejected 197 motions on energy policy, while also issuing reports on nuclear power licensing, wind power in municipalities, and the renewable energy directive.

What Mattered Most

The social insurance qualification reform (Prop. 2025/26:136) stands as the week's most consequential development. By requiring immigrants to earn eligibility for Swedish welfare benefits, the government is fundamentally reshaping the relationship between immigration and the welfare state. Coming just days after the stricter deportation announcement, it represents the clearest expression yet of the coalition's core policy thesis: that rights must follow responsibilities. The opposition will seek to frame this as an attack on universalism; the government will argue it safeguards it. The SfU committee review will be the arena where this defining battle of the session plays out.
