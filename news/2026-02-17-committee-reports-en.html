A register for all condominiums

Committee: Civil Affairs Committee (Civilutskottet)

Jurisdiction: civil law, consumer protection, housing, insolvency, enforcement

Document: CU28

The Civil Affairs Committee proposes establishing a comprehensive national registry for all housing cooperatives (bostadsrätter), addressing a critical transparency gap in Sweden's housing market. Currently, information about condominium associations is fragmented across multiple municipal and private databases, creating opportunities for fraud and making due diligence difficult for buyers. The proposed central register would include ownership structures, financial health indicators, and governance documentation for all 1.3 million cooperative housing units. This reform responds to several high-profile cases of financial mismanagement in housing cooperatives and aligns with broader digitalization initiatives. The committee expects implementation to reduce transaction costs while improving consumer protection in a market segment representing 40% of Swedish housing stock.

Extra budget amendment for 2026 – Support to Ukraine and vaccine preparedness

Committee: Finance Committee (Finansutskottet)

Jurisdiction: taxation, budget, financial markets, currency

Document: FiU46

The Finance Committee recommends approval of a supplementary budget for 2026 allocating resources to two critical priorities: continued military and humanitarian support for Ukraine, and strengthened pandemic preparedness through expanded vaccine manufacturing capacity. The proposal includes €450 million in additional Ukraine aid (bringing total 2026 support to €1.8 billion) and €180 million for domestic vaccine production infrastructure. The committee majority, comprising the government coalition, frames this as responsible crisis management balancing international solidarity with national security interests. Opposition parties generally support Ukraine aid but criticize vaccine spending as belated response to lessons from COVID-19. The supplementary budget reflects Sweden's evolving security posture following NATO accession and recognition of strategic vulnerability in pharmaceutical supply chains. Fiscal hawks note the additional spending increases the 2026 deficit to 0.8% of GDP, testing the government's commitment to balanced budgets amid multiple security challenges.

The Environment and Agriculture Committee addresses multiple animal welfare proposals from the 2025 general motion period, rejecting most while acknowledging ongoing policy development. The majority position—supported by the government coalition—argues that Sweden already maintains some of Europe's strictest animal welfare standards and that recent EU harmonization efforts require a wait-and-see approach. Opposition parties, particularly the Green Party and Left Party, criticize this as insufficient given growing public concern about factory farming practices. The committee signals openness to targeted reforms once the EU's revised Animal Welfare Regulation takes effect in 2027, prioritizing regulatory stability over immediate legislative action.

Abolishing the notification requirement before parental benefit applications

Committee: Social Affairs Committee (Socialutskottet)

Jurisdiction: healthcare, social services, family policy, parental benefits

Document: SfU20

The Social Affairs Committee recommends eliminating the advance notification requirement for parental leave applications, streamlining Sweden's famously generous but administratively complex parental benefit system. Currently, parents must notify the Social Insurance Agency weeks before applying for benefits, creating unnecessary bureaucratic hurdles. The reform leverages digitalization advances that allow real-time processing of applications, eliminating the historical justification for advance notice. This change affects approximately 110,000 families annually and is projected to save 15,000 administrative hours. The committee frames this as modernizing Sweden's welfare system while maintaining its core generosity, though opposition parties argue for more fundamental reforms to increase flexibility in benefit use.

Future data protection at the Tax Agency, Customs and Enforcement Authority

Committee: Taxation Committee (Skatteutskottet)

Jurisdiction: tax law, tax administration, customs

Document: SkU10

The Taxation Committee addresses data protection frameworks for three agencies handling vast quantities of sensitive personal and financial information: the Tax Agency (Skatteverket), Customs (Tullverket), and the Enforcement Authority (Kronofogdemyndigheten). The report responds to the Data Protection Authority's critical review identifying gaps in current safeguards, particularly regarding algorithmic decision-making, data retention periods, and third-party access controls. The committee recommends enhanced technical measures: encryption standards, access logging, automated risk flagging, and regular security audits. Politically sensitive aspects include balancing law enforcement needs (tax evasion detection, smuggling prevention) against privacy rights and potential discrimination risks from automated profiling. Opposition parties advocate stronger parliamentary oversight of agency data practices, while the government majority emphasizes operational flexibility. The reform timeline targets full implementation by 2027, addressing EU criticism that Sweden's public sector digitalization has outpaced privacy protections.

Controls of cash at the internal border

Committee: Taxation Committee (Skatteutskottet)

Jurisdiction: tax law, tax administration, customs

Document: SkU19

The Taxation Committee recommends implementing systematic cash control regimes at Sweden's borders with Denmark and Norway, responding to Schengen-area obligations to combat money laundering and terrorist financing. The proposal authorizes Customs officers to inspect and register cash amounts exceeding €10,000, aligning Swedish practice with EU regulations that many member states already enforce. The committee acknowledges tensions between free movement principles and financial crime prevention, noting that organized crime networks exploit regulatory gaps between jurisdictions. Implementation details include traveler declaration requirements, confiscation procedures for non-compliance, and data-sharing protocols with European financial intelligence units. Civil liberties advocates express concerns about potential ethnic profiling and disproportionate impact on migrant communities transferring remittances. The government argues controls target criminal proceeds rather than legitimate cross-border activities, citing intelligence indicating hundreds of millions in illicit cash flows annually. The reform reflects Sweden's integration into broader European security architecture despite traditional Nordic openness.
