Nine Government Propositions Signal Broad Reform Agenda

Sweden's government tables nine propositions covering weapons law, VAT fraud, financial crisis management, climate policy, police reform and data protection in a sweeping legislative push

The Swedish government tabled nine propositions on February 17th, 2026, spanning weapons legislation, VAT fraud countermeasures, financial-sector crisis management, climate-policy accountability, a police-reform audit and data-protection modernisation. The legislative burst underscores Prime Minister Ulf Kristersson's determination to advance a broad domestic agenda despite the election calendar tightening.

Legislative Overview

The nine propositions tabled on February 17th cover multiple policy domains, with the Ministry of Justice and Ministry of Finance each sponsoring several bills.

Key Propositions

  • The Swedish NAO's Report on the State's Work with Climate Policy Framework (HD03122)
  • Regulations on Prohibition of Use and Possession of Certain Animal Medicines (HD03120)
  • Measures Against VAT Fraud (HD03128)
  • A New Weapons Law (HD03141)
  • Disclosure Obligations for Certain E-Identification Companies (HD03126)
  • A New Function for Operational Crisis Management in the Financial Sector (HD03116)
  • The Swedish NAO's Report on the 2015 Police Reform (HD03110)
  • Disclosure of Information from the Register of Beneficial Owners (HD03129)
  • Explosive Goods – Improved Control Opportunities (HD03123)

Political Analysis

The breadth of this legislative package—spanning public safety, fiscal integrity, climate accountability and civil-service transparency—reflects a government keen to demonstrate policy delivery ahead of the 2026 election. The new weapons law and VAT fraud measures address voter concerns about security and economic fairness, while the climate-policy and police-reform audits provide institutional accountability. The financial crisis-management bill signals awareness of systemic risks in a volatile global economy.

Outlook

Committee referrals are expected to be confirmed by the Speaker in coming days, with the weapons law and VAT fraud propositions likely to draw the most debate. Opposition parties have already signalled motions on several of these proposals, setting the stage for substantive legislative bargaining in the spring session.
