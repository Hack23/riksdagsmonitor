```html
<h3>A New Weapons Law (HD03141)</h3>
<p><strong>Document:</strong> <a href="...">HD03141</a> | 
   <strong>Ministry:</strong> Justice | 
   <strong>Submitted:</strong> 17 February 2026</p>

<p><strong>Policy Analysis:</strong> The government proposes Sweden's first 
comprehensive weapons law modernization since the 1990s, consolidating 12 
existing firearms regulations into a unified legislative framework. The reform 
responds directly to rising gang violence concerns and aligns with Tidö Agreement 
commitments to strengthen police enforcement powers.</p>

<p><strong>Key Changes:</strong> The legislation introduces stricter licensing 
requirements for semi-automatic weapons, expands police authority to conduct 
unannounced inspections of licensed firearms owners, and establishes a centralized 
digital weapons register. Penalties for illegal weapons possession increase from 
maximum 2 years to 4 years imprisonment, while organized firearms trafficking 
becomes eligible for up to 10 years.</p>

<p><strong>Who's Affected:</strong> Approximately 580,000 licensed firearms owners 
face new documentation requirements and inspection protocols. Hunting organizations 
have raised concerns about administrative burdens, while sports shooting federations 
broadly support modernization efforts. Police estimate 3,500 illegally held weapons 
currently in circulation could be targeted under enhanced enforcement provisions.</p>

<p><strong>Political Context:</strong> This represents a core Tidö Agreement 
deliverable, with strong support from Moderates, Christian Democrats, and Sweden 
Democrats. The Liberal party has expressed reservations about expanded police 
powers, potentially requiring negotiations. The Social Democrats' response remains 
critical—their cooperation could enable broader parliamentary backing, but early 
signals suggest opposition to surveillance expansion elements.</p>

<p><strong>Implementation Timeline:</strong> The proposition goes to the 
Constitutional Committee (KU) for initial review, expected March 2026. Full 
parliamentary debate likely scheduled for May-June 2026, with enforcement beginning 
January 2027 if passed. The 18-month transition period allows firearms owners to 
comply with new registration requirements.</p>

<p><strong>Budget Impact:</strong> Initial costs estimated at 280 million SEK 
for digital register development and police training (2026-2027), with ongoing 
annual costs of 45 million SEK for system maintenance and enforcement. Finance 
Ministry proposes funding through justice sector reallocation rather than new 
appropriations.</p>
```
