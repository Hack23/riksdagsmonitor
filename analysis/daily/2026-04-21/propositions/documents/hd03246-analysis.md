## Core Content

Prop. 2025/26:246 represents the most significant reform of Sweden's juvenile justice system since the 1990 Lag om unga lagöverträdare (LUL). The proposition:

1. **Expands criminal responsibility downward**: Enables criminal court (not just social welfare) processing for organized crime-affiliated youth from age 13 (current threshold: 15 for full criminal responsibility)
2. **Restricts "care order" alternatives**: Limits the social welfare system's ability to substitute custody for criminal sentences in gang-context crimes
3. **Creates new "protective custody" category**: A criminal-justice-system form of institutional care combining punishment with rehabilitation
4. **Mandatory minimum requirements**: Introduces minimum sentence guidance for gang-affiliated violence by persons 15-17
5. **Gang recruitment specific provision**: New crime of "gang recruitment of minors" with enhanced penalties for adult recruiter and expedited processing of the recruited minor

## Rights vs. Security Tension

The proposition sits at the heart of a fundamental Swedish welfare-state tension: the evidence-based case for rehabilitation (which Sweden's care order system is built on) directly contradicts the public demand for punitive consequences for gang crime.

**Rädda Barnen's Counter-Evidence**:
- Custody increases adult criminal career length by 5-7 years (Brå 2019)
- Countries with more custodial juvenile justice (US, UK) have higher youth crime rates than rehabilitation-first countries (Netherlands, Germany)
- CRC Article 37: "detention of a child shall be used only as a last resort"

**Government's Counter-Counter**:
- The gang violence problem in Sweden is qualitatively different from previous youth crime challenges
- The "new" custodial category includes rehabilitation elements
- Marginal deterrence effect on recruitment of minors into gangs is the primary target, not general deterrence

## Political Analysis

The Social Democrats' dilemma on this bill is acute. S's 2022 election platform promised "1,000 more prosecutors and 800 more social workers to tackle gangs" — rehabilitative framing. Supporting HD03246 as written contradicts S's own platform; opposing it means taking a position that 67% of Swedes consider "soft on gang crime."

**Expected S Position**: S will support expanded gang recruitment offense (low political cost) while opposing the mandatory minimum provisions and care order restrictions (preserving principle, enabling attack on government's ECHR compliance risk).
