## Core Content

Prop. 2025/26:242 delivers the most comprehensive reform of Sweden's forestry regulation since the 1993 Skogsvårdslagen (Forestry Act). The proposition:

1. **Clarity Principle**: Establishes that "active forestry management is the baseline" — operations must only be restricted when specific habitat protection evidence justifies intervention
2. **Compensation Entitlement**: Forest owners restricted by artskydd (species protection) or Natura 2000 designations receive statutory right to market-value compensation (not just "reasonable" compensation)
3. **Burden of Proof Shift**: Transfers burden from forest owner to prove operations are harmless, to authorities to prove operations are harmful
4. **Time-Limited Permit Process**: MJU-aligned permits with maximum 8-month processing time (current: 18-24 months average)
5. **Thinning Presumption**: General thinning and selective clearing presumed permitted unless specific species evidence forbids it

## Political Analysis

**The LRF Delivery**: Peter Kullgren's proposition is the culmination of years of intensive LRF (Federation of Swedish Farmers) lobbying. The "active forestry" framework inverts the regulatory default from "restriction unless proven safe" to "permitted unless proven harmful" — a fundamental property rights philosophy shift.

**EU Tension**: Sweden's forest industry produces roughly 25% of total Swedish export revenues. The proposition attempts to protect this economic asset while maintaining nominal compliance with EU Habitats Directive Article 6. The risk is that "clarification" looks like "derogation" to Commission DG Environment — particularly given Sweden's three pending infringement cases.

**Electoral Geography**: Forest constituencies are disproportionately in Sweden's northern and central regions (Norrland, Dalarna, Värmland, Västra Götaland north). These are swing regions where M and SD compete. Delivering this forestry bill consolidates M's rural base while making it harder for SD to claim monopoly on rural Sweden.

## Election 2026 Implications

**Electoral Impact**: 🟧 MEDIUM-HIGH  
**Confidence**: 🟩 HIGH  
The forestry framework is not a top-3 voter issue nationally but is highly salient in rural constituencies. It demonstrates that the Tidö coalition serves rural Sweden's economic interests — a consolidation play rather than a persuasion play.

**Policy Legacy**: If the EU infringement risk materializes post-election, a new government would face the uncomfortable choice of defending a legally risky policy or backtracking on promises to LRF constituents.
