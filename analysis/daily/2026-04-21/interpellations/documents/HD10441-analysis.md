## Summary

Elsa Widding (independent) escalates a constitutional rule-of-law challenge targeting systemic self-policing within Sweden's legal profession. Her prior written question (2025/26:503, 2026-02-06) asked whether Strömmer had analyzed legal certainty in four domains where lawyers exclusively judge other lawyers: civil disputes, Special Prosecutor Chamber for judicial misconduct, Bar Association self-discipline, and JK oversight of Bar Association decisions. Strömmer's Feb 18 response cited existing jäv rules and JO/JK oversight as sufficient — without commissioning any new analysis. Widding now escalates to an interpellation demanding specific yes/no answers on whether analysis HAS BEEN DONE for each of the four domains.

## Political Analysis

**Systemic Constitutional Issue**: Widding is raising a structural rule-of-law question about the Swedish justice system: can a system where lawyers exclusively review lawyers for misconduct be considered impartial? This is a constitutional-quality question touching Article 1 of the Regeringsform (equal treatment, objectivity, impartiality). The fact that Strömmer's answer was "existing rules are sufficient" without any analytical backing is the political opening Widding exploits.

**Why Widding Filed This (Non-Partisan Dimension)**: As an independent MP with no party affiliation, Widding's interpellation demonstrates that rule-of-law concerns transcend party politics. Her challenge is harder for Strömmer to dismiss as opposition party tactics — it requires substantive engagement with the constitutional question. This makes it more newsworthy in legal/media circles.

**Strömmer's Strategic Position**: Strömmer faces four parallel yes/no questions — for each procedural domain, has he ordered analysis? If the answer to all four is "no," then his position is that existing rules are sufficient without any systematic review. If "yes," he must release the analysis. Either answer carries political costs: "no" looks complacent; "yes" must defend the content.

**Previous Pattern**: Widding has previously asked about related legal certainty issues; Strömmer has a pattern of citing existing legal frameworks as responses without conducting new empirical analysis. This interpellation documents that pattern for election-year accountability use.
