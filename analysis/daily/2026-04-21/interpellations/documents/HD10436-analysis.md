## Significance Score: 5.3 / 10

Lower-tier in raw score but strategically important — Sweden's space industry is both an industrial-policy and a total-defence story. Esrange Space Center (Kiruna) is one of Europe's leading launch/sounding-rocket facilities, and the EU is restructuring its space policy (IRIS², GOVSATCOM, Secure Connectivity). S asks whether Sweden is capitalising on the strategic moment or losing ground to Norway (Andøya), the UK (SaxaVord, Sutherland), and EU industrial programmes.

## Summary

The interpellation asks Edholm (a) what industrial-policy measures will accompany Rymdstrategin, (b) whether Esrange commercial-launch ambitions (including small-satellite orbital launches) are being supported with co-funding, regulatory clarity (Rymdlag 2023:796) and export-control coordination, (c) how Sweden is positioning within the EU IRIS² Secure Connectivity programme, and (d) whether research funding (Rymdstyrelsen, VR, Vinnova, ESA contribution) is adequate.

## Political Analysis

**L-minister, technical portfolio**: Edholm's primary profile is school-policy, not industrial/space. She is unlikely to announce major new measures unilaterally — any substantial answer will be coordinated with näringsdepartementet and finansdepartementet. This suggests a procedural or deferring response.

**Strategic urgency**: Sweden has a genuine, geographically-enabled competitive advantage in space (northern latitude, sparsely populated inland terrain, established SSC infrastructure). Letting Norway or the UK capture first-mover orbital-launch advantage would be a historic lost opportunity. S frames the interpellation as industrial-policy ambition the coalition allegedly lacks.

**Total-defence dimension**: Post-NATO accession, Sweden's access to space-based communications, ISR and position-navigation-timing is strategically critical. EU IRIS² and national-security-of-space programmes are the operational layer. Under-investment is therefore also a defence posture question.

**Research-funding linkage**: Rymdstyrelsen, VR and Vinnova all feed space-sector industry capacity. Budget tightness in 2024–26 has constrained calls. The interpellation allows S to tie general research-funding critique to a high-profile strategic sector.

**Regulatory follow-through**: The 2023 rymdlag created a framework but delegated many technical rules. Industry actors report uncertainty over insurance, liability, export-control coordination with ISP. Edholm's response is a chance to clarify — or, more likely, to commit to a forthcoming regulatory package.
