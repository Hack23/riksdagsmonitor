## Summary

Sweden faces an acute crisis in occupational physician supply. The government has failed to designate a responsible authority for ST-training in occupational medicine following a chain of institutional decisions: Arbetslivsinstitutet closed 2007; Myndigheten för arbetsmiljökunskap commissioned 2021, reported 2024 (recommend Försäkringskassan as responsible), then itself closed; tasks moved to Arbetsmiljöverket and KI — but KI explicitly rejected responsibility, and no budget was allocated. The question has been shuffled between three ministries (Arbetsmarknadsdepartementet, Socialdepartementet, Utbildningsdepartementet) without resolution.

## Political Analysis

**Why This Matters**: The occupational physician shortage is not abstract — it threatens the legal basis of workplace safety inspections for hazardous occupations (asbestos, lead, chemical exposure, night work). When companies cannot find company doctors for mandatory health checks, they either break the law or workers lose protection. This is a compounding institutional failure: the government's own 2021 commission found the status quo unsustainable in 2024, then the commissioning agency was itself abolished without implementing its recommendation.

**Accountability Structure**: Haraldsson has cornered Britz in a bureaucratic trap. The previous interpellation with the health minister resulted in the health minister pointing to the labor market minister as responsible — Haraldsson now has that on record and is holding Britz to account. Britz cannot escape responsibility by claiming it belongs to another ministry without contradicting the on-record position taken during the previous debate.

**Election 2026 Vulnerability**: The slow-motion dismantling of occupational health infrastructure since 2007 fits S's "government doesn't protect workers" narrative. The specific finding that the generation of company doctors is retiring with no replacement is highly media-transmissible. This affects small businesses (who cannot afford private occupational health) and public sector employers (hospitals, schools) that rely on the public occupational health system.

## SWOT Contribution

- **Government W**: Institutional vacuum in occupational medicine training — no ministry has accepted responsibility since 2024 report
- **Opposition S**: Clear evidence chain: government commissioned review → review found problem → government closed the review body → problem unsolved
- **Workers/Citizens W**: Legal mandatory health checks at risk; workforce aging without replacement; employers potentially in breach of Arbetsmiljöverket regulations
- **Business T**: SME liability exposure if mandatory medical checks cannot be performed; healthcare employers especially at risk
