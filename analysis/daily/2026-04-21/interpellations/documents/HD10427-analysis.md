## Significance Score: 5.7 / 10

Lower-tier politically but operationally important: PostNord is a Swedish-Danish state-owned entity providing the universal postal service and operating under the Postal Services Act (Postlagen 2010:1045). The interpellation tests whether the Finance Minister will treat PostNord's performance issues as an ownership-governance failure or as a market/structural inevitability.

## Summary

Opposition questions whether the state, as PostNord's majority Swedish owner (60% Sweden / 40% Denmark), is discharging its ownership responsibilities — particularly given repeated delivery-quality failures, deteriorating financial results, governance turnover, and the universal-service obligation imposed by Postlagen. The interpellation asks whether Svantesson intends to update the ownership directive, strengthen board requirements, or initiate a strategic review.

## Political Analysis

**Ownership-policy lever**: The Finance Minister has direct, uncontested authority over the ownership directive. Any answer that says "this is a board matter" is weak — because the owner sets the board's remit. S frames this as ministerial avoidance of governance responsibility.

**Cross-border governance complexity**: PostNord's bi-national ownership makes unilateral Swedish action difficult — but Sweden, as majority owner, has the decisive vote. This subtlety often shields ministers from accountability; the interpellation forces Svantesson to articulate Sweden's actual leverage.

**Rural and elderly service angle**: Delivery degradation disproportionately affects rural constituents (reinforcing C/KD narratives) and elderly households (reinforcing SPF/PRO/S advocacy). The interpellation sits on a cross-party salience pattern even if filed by S.

**Market liberalisation tension**: The 2013 postal-market reform removed PostNord's letter-monopoly; the universal service obligation remained but revenue base eroded. S is asking whether the ownership policy has adapted — indirectly reopening the structural debate.
