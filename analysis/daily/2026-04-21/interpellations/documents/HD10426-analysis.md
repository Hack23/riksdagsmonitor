## Summary

The interpellation confronts the Foreign Minister with Israel's expansion of capital-punishment legislation (including proposals applicable to security offences and to individuals — including minors — tried in military courts in the occupied territories). The question set asks whether the government (a) has conveyed concerns to Israel, (b) will act within the EU/UN framework, and (c) considers Swedish arms-export and development-cooperation policies consistent with the new legal situation.

## Political Analysis

**Foreign-policy consistency trap**: Sweden's official position — that the death penalty is never acceptable, under any circumstances — is near-universal across Swedish parties. Malmer Stenergard cannot soften that position without a domestic political cost. But criticising Israel publicly creates friction with the government's post-October-7 alignment and with the Tidö agreement's general stance. The interpellation weaponises this tension.

**The minor-defendant angle**: If the legislation applies to persons under 18 tried in military courts, Sweden's commitments under the UN Convention on the Rights of the Child (CRC), to which Israel is also a party, are directly engaged. This converts the debate from diplomatic posturing into a legally framed accountability demand.

**EU coordination dimension**: The opposition will ask whether Sweden has used its voice within the EU Foreign Affairs Council (FAC) or PSC to raise the legislation. A non-answer ("bilateral diplomatic channels") reads as passivity; a specific answer commits to visible EU action.
