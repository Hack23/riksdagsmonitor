## Summary

SD confronts the Justice Minister on whether the government's forthcoming bill (prop. 2025/26:133 — criminal-law or media-law reform) meets constitutional freedom-of-expression guarantees. SD is not a traditional civil-liberties party; its use of yttrandefrihetsgrundlagen (YGL) and tryckfrihetsförordningen (TF) framing signals a tactical move — using constitutional protections to slow or shape a Tidö-coalition law package whose scope SD either wants softened or re-targeted. The interpellation forces Strömmer to demonstrate explicit Lagrådet (Council on Legislation) review, to disclose any concurrent review the Chancellor of Justice (JK) or Justitieombudsmannen (JO) has noted, and to address international comparisons.

## Significance Score: 6.2 / 10

Constitutional-law interpellations often rank moderately, but this one is elevated because (a) it comes from within the Tidö-adjacent coalition (SD is the parliamentary supporter, not a cabinet party), (b) it concerns a named government bill rather than an abstract question, and (c) it touches Swedish press-freedom traditions with near-sacred status domestically.

## Political Analysis

**Intra-coalition manoeuvre**: An interpellation from SD to a cabinet member (M) is politically unusual — it signals public distance from a specific bill. SD's base partly overlaps with constituencies who mistrust speech-regulation expansion (either on grounds of anti-establishment media use, or online expression). By filing publicly, SD protects itself from charges of silent complicity.

**Constitutional-law credibility play**: SD has historically struggled with civil-libertarian credibility. Appealing to TF/YGL gives SD a posture of constitutional guardianship. It also mirrors critiques that media commentators and some legal academics have raised — allowing SD to credit-claim if the bill is narrowed.

**Strömmer's options**:
1. Reassure — "bill fully complies, Lagrådet approved" → commits the government to Lagrådet's language
2. Defer — "Lagrådet review is ongoing" → admits the bill is not yet ready
3. Reinterpret — "the bill is narrower than critics suggest" → narrows the scope in a binding parliamentary-record way

All three paths move the bill in SD's preferred direction.

**Media-ecosystem stakes**: Swedish investigative journalism — including outlets that regularly cover SD critically — depends on the full scope of TF/YGL protections (anonymous sourcing, meddelarfrihet, censurförbud). A restrictive bill would be opposed by the same media critical of SD, creating an unusual tactical alliance.
