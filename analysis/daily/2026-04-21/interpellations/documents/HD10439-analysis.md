## Full Question Analysis

**The core question to Minister Strömmer**:
> "When does the Minister and the government intend to present an action plan to ensure more police in Stockholm?"

**What prompted this interpellation**:
BRÅ (National Crime Prevention Council) published its evaluation of the 10,000-new-police milestone in late March 2026. The evaluation confirmed the target was reached nationally, but revealed that Stockholm is the **only police region where police density is declining in relation to population growth**. The region:
- Houses nearly 25% of Sweden's population
- Contains approximately 50% of Sweden's vulnerable housing areas
- Is home to parliament, government buildings, and major embassies
- Hosts major national and international events
- Has been severely affected by organized violence over the past 15 years

The interpellation notes Stockholm is approximately **1,000 officers short** compared to what proportional growth in other regions would imply.

## Political Intelligence Assessment

**Government's dilemma**: The BRÅ data is authoritative and non-partisan. Strömmer cannot dispute the finding. His response options are limited to:
1. Acknowledge the gap and announce a plan → validates opposition framing
2. Deflect to municipal/regional police priorities → risks appearing indifferent to capital's needs
3. Frame it as "phase 2" of policing strategy → buys time but signals no immediate action

**S's strategic objective**: Create a concrete, quotable contrast between the "10,000 police success" headline and the reality facing Stockholm residents. In Election 2026 campaign materials, this becomes: "Government claimed success while Stockholm remains 1,000 police officers short."

**EU/International dimension**: Stockholm's security is a national security issue, not just a city issue — parliament, government, embassies, and NATO-related infrastructure are all located there.
