## Summary

SD asks Forssmed whether the government will (a) withdraw or block state subsidies to religious communities that host or tolerate hate-speech content, (b) tighten SST's assessment framework, and (c) coordinate with Säkerhetspolisen (SÄPO) on threat-propagating environments. The interpellation cites investigative reporting and named incidents where sermons or adjacent activities contained threat or hate content.

## Political Analysis

**KD's dilemma**: KD traditionally defends religious-community autonomy (historically church-rooted) but is also the minister party at Socialdepartementet, whose portfolio includes SST. Forssmed must balance (a) SD pressure to be tough, (b) religious-freedom constitutional commitments, and (c) due-process standards for subsidy decisions. Any blanket policy risks EU/ECHR challenge.

**SÄPO coordination limits**: SÄPO intelligence typically cannot drive administrative sanctions directly — due-process law (förvaltningslagen) requires disclosable evidence. The interpellation implicitly pushes Forssmed toward either (a) changed evidentiary standards for subsidy withdrawal, or (b) a publicly articulated cooperation model with SÄPO that respects rule-of-law limits.

**Multi-community equity**: A policy narrowly targeting mosques would face both legal and political counter-pressure. A generally applicable framework (all religious communities, any hate-speech association) is harder to attack but also harder to apply swiftly. Forssmed is likely to frame his response in general-applicability terms.

**Election-year positioning**: SD's interpellation provides its base with a documented, on-record demand before September 2026. Whatever Forssmed answers, SD gains — either a win ("government moving in our direction") or a grievance ("KD blocks needed measures").
