## Summary

Kallifatides (S) asks Svantesson (a) whether the government will initiate a broad tax review covering income tax, capital taxation, property/wealth taxation, corporate tax, VAT and environmental taxes, (b) whether the government acknowledges the "skattetryckets sammansättning" (composition of the tax burden) has drifted since the 1990 reform in ways that weaken both efficiency and legitimacy, and (c) what barriers (coalition politics, Tidö-avtalet constraints, timetable) the government sees.

## Political Analysis

**Why Svantesson resists**: A broad review invites cross-party negotiation — reducing the government's unilateral control over tax-policy narrative. It also opens ideologically sensitive files (wealth tax, property tax, capital-income integration) that the M/KD/L/SD coalition prefers to keep closed.

**Why S pushes**: The 1990 analogue is politically potent — S branded itself as the party of consensus-driven, modernising tax reform. A refusal by Svantesson lets S argue "Moderaterna blockerar stor reform av rädsla för öppen granskning" (M blocks broad reform out of fear of open scrutiny). It also pre-positions S for a potential post-election government role where a cross-party tax commission would be a signature commitment.

**Economist alignment**: Most Swedish tax economists agree the system is overdue for structural review. Expert consensus is unusual in Swedish policy and gives the opposition a credibility advantage.

**The composition-of-tax-burden angle**: Marginal income tax on labour (including payroll taxes) is high by international standards; capital-income taxation is often lower effective rate than labour income; property taxation is unusually constrained. These distortions interact with housing, labour-market mobility and entrepreneurship debates.

**Tidö-avtalet constraint**: The coalition agreement contains tax-specific commitments (ROT expansion, specific rate paths). A broad review would expose these as outcomes of a political deal rather than principled design — another reason for government resistance.
