## Executive Summary

Proposition 2025/26:246 represents Sweden's most significant criminal justice reform targeting youth offenders in decades. The government proposes **lowering the age of criminal responsibility from 15 to 13** for serious crimes (a temporary five-year measure), **abolishing sentencing discounts for 18-20 year olds**, and **increasing maximum prison sentences for under-18s to 18 years**. Signed by PM Kristersson and Justice Minister Strömmer on April 16, 2026, with proposed implementation on August 2, 2026.

This proposition is a direct policy response to escalating gang violence involving younger perpetrators. It represents a fundamental shift in Sweden's historically rehabilitation-focused approach to youth crime toward a more punitive framework.

## SWOT Analysis

### Strengths
- **Coalition unanimity**: M+KD+L government with SD parliamentary support ensures passage (176+ seats out of 349)
- **Public mandate**: Strong public support for stricter youth crime measures
- **Temporal safeguard**: 5-year sunset clause on age reduction addresses proportionality concerns
- **Comprehensive scope**: Addresses full spectrum from age limits to supervision enforcement

### Weaknesses
- **Constitutional tension**: Lowering criminal age to 13 challenges UN Convention on the Rights of the Child (incorporated into Swedish law 2020)
- **Implementation capacity**: SiS (Statens institutionsstyrelse) and Kriminalvården face severe capacity shortages
- **Evidence base**: Limited empirical evidence that lower criminal age deters youth gang involvement
- **Legal complexity**: 31 separate law amendments required for implementation

### Opportunities
- **Election positioning**: Strong law and order credentials for coalition ahead of 2026 election
- **Cross-bloc potential**: S has signaled conditional support for some measures
- **Systemic reform**: Opens door for comprehensive juvenile justice modernization

### Threats
- **International criticism**: UN Committee on the Rights of the Child likely to issue formal objection
- **Constitutional review**: Potential Lagrådet referral for proportionality assessment
- **Recidivism risk**: Younger incarceration may increase long-term criminal careers
- **V+MP opposition**: Strong ideological opposition may polarize debate further
