## Cross-Party Convergence Analysis

### Convergence Point 1: Prop. 235 (Deportation) — 3-Party Opposition
V (full rejection) + C (threshold) + MP (selective) = maximum scrutiny at SfU
- NOT unified bloc — each party has incompatible demands
- Combined effect: SfU must justify its recommendations in writing against three distinct legal objections
- Historical precedent: When multiple parties file on same prop with different strategies, committee chair typically seeks rapporteur reconciliation [MEDIUM confidence]

### Convergence Point 2: Prop. 228 (Arms) — V+MP Convergence
- V: Reject entire proposition
- MP: Accept framework, demand dictatorships/warring states clauses
- Convergence area: Both parties oppose arms to authoritarian states
- Key difference: V's full rejection may undermine MP's targeted ask in UU committee
- Prior precedent: UU has historically accepted "declaration" motions on arms export human rights criteria without binding legislation [MEDIUM confidence]

### Convergence Point 3: Prop. 236 (Fuel Tax) — V+S Cross-Bloc Opposition
- V filed mot. 4092 (Apr 16) opposing fuel tax cut
- S filed mot. 4082 (Apr 15) opposing fuel tax cut with different rationale
- UNUSUAL: V and S opposition to same fiscal measure signals broad left-centre coalition against this specific SD-influenced policy
- Important: This does NOT mean V+S form a governing alternative — they disagree on virtually everything else
- Media narrative potential: "Even Social Democrats oppose government's fuel tax giveaway"

### Convergence Point 4: Prop. 216 (Healthcare) — C+S+V Opposition
- C (mot. 4094), S (mot. 4081), V (mot. 4083) all filed motions against healthcare proposition from different angles
- Government faces near-total SoU opposition to prop. 216 changes to HSL
