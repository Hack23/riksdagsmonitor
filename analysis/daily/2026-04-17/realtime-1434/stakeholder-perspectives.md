# Stakeholder Perspectives — Realtime Monitor 1434
**Date**: 2026-04-17 | **Focus**: Ukraine Accountability Package + Constitutional Amendments

---

## 1. Citizens / Swedish Public

**Position**: Strong support for Ukraine (polling consistently 60-70%+ support for Ukraine aid)
**Impact of Ukraine propositions**: Direct alignment with public sentiment — Sweden is "doing the right thing"
**Impact of constitutional amendments**: Limited public awareness of KU32/KU33 specifics; media/press freedom advocates will be more engaged
**Impact of housing reforms**: Direct impact on ~2M bostadsrätter owners; generally positive reaction to transparency improvements
**Electoral implications**: Ukraine solidarity is a cross-party consensus that no politician wants to be seen opposing

---

## 2. Government Coalition (M/KD/L)

**Position**: Strongly supportive of all measures — proposing them
**Narrative advantage**: "Sweden is punching above its weight in Ukraine accountability" — good for PM Kristersson and FM Stenergard's international reputation
**Risk**: If Ukraine fatigue sets in, having committed early could be politically difficult; but current trajectory is more not less engagement
**Key individuals**: 
- **Ulf Kristersson** (M, PM): Co-signed both Ukraine propositions; stakes his foreign policy legacy on NATO+Ukraine
- **Maria Malmer Stenergard** (M, FM): Direct champion; Nuremberg framing is her political capital investment
- **Erik Slottner** (KD, Minister for Civil Affairs): Responsible for HD03244 (data sharing)

---

## 3. Opposition Bloc (S/V/MP)

**Social Democrats (S)**:
- Ukraine: Strongly supportive — S led Sweden's initial Ukraine response under PM Magdalena Andersson
- Constitutional KU33: May raise press freedom concerns (S has traditionally strong press freedom commitment)
- Housing: Supportive of consumer protection measures

**V (Left Party)**:
- Ukraine: Supportive of accountability measures, though historically skeptical of NATO/military framing
- Constitutional: Critical of KU33 — likely to highlight transparency concerns
- Housing: Supportive of tenant protection elements

**MP (Greens)**:
- Ukraine: Strong support — international law and human rights align with MP values
- Constitutional KU32: Enthusiastic — EU accessibility compliance and disability rights
- Housing: Positive framing on market transparency

---

## 4. Business & Industry

**Real estate sector**: Broadly supportive of condominium register (reduces market uncertainty)
**Banks/Mortgage lenders**: Supportive — cleaner pledge/mortgage registration system reduces risk
**Defense industry**: Neutral on legal accountability measures; benefits from general Ukraine support maintaining procurement commitments
**Tech sector**: Interest in data interoperability (HD03244) — creates standards compliance requirements

---

## 5. Civil Society

**War crimes accountability NGOs** (e.g., Amnesty Sweden, Human Rights Watch Sweden): 
- Enthusiastically supportive of Ukraine tribunal and compensation commission
- Will advocate for full Riksdag approval

**Press freedom organizations** (TU, Utgivarna):
- Cautious on KU33 — monitoring for press freedom implications of removing seized materials from public record status
- Will seek clarity on investigative journalism protections

**Disability rights organizations**:
- KU32 is a victory — long-sought accessibility rights in media

---

## 6. International Actors & EU

**Ukraine (Zelensky government)**: Central proponent; convention signed at diplomatic conference Dec 16, 2025 with Zelensky present
**Council of Europe**: Framework body for the Special Tribunal; Sweden's membership strengthens the institution
**EU institutions**: Supportive — EU has been lead driver of Ukraine accountability measures; Sweden's membership aligns with EU foreign policy
**NATO allies**: Positive — demonstrates Sweden's commitment to rules-based order as new NATO member
**Russia**: Hostile — views all accountability mechanisms as illegal; likely to respond with rhetoric and potentially hybrid measures
**US**: Position uncertain under current administration — has been ambiguous on ICC/international tribunals

---

## 7. Judiciary & Constitutional Bodies

**Lagrådet (Council on Legislation)**: Will examine constitutional amendments for compliance
**Constitutional Court (no formal equivalent in Sweden)**: KU itself performs constitutional review function
**International Criminal Court (ICC)**: Complementary relationship — the Special Tribunal fills the gap in ICC's aggression jurisdiction
**Riksdagens ombudsman (JO)**: No direct role but will monitor implementation

---

## 8. Media & Public Opinion

**Swedish mainstream media** (DN, SvD, Aftonbladet, Expressen):
- Ukraine tribunal story is newsworthy — Nuremberg comparison will be headline fodder
- Constitutional amendments will generate debate on press freedom (KU33)
- Housing register: consumer/economy story, secondary coverage

**International media**: Ukraine tribunal story has global newsworthiness; will be picked up by international wire services
**Social media dynamics**: Ukraine solidarity messaging performs well; expect positive engagement on tribunal story
