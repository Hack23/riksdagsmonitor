# Per-Document Analysis: HD03246

**Title**: Skärpta regler för unga lagöverträdare  
**Prop. Number**: 2025/26:246  
**Minister**: Gunnar Strömmer (M, Justitiedepartementet)  
**Committee**: Justitieutskottet (JuU)  
**Date**: 2026-04-16  
**Significance**: 9/10 🟦 VERY HIGH

## Policy Summary

Proposition 2025/26:246 introduces the most significant overhaul of Swedish juvenile justice since the 1964 Youth Offender Act. Key proposals include:

1. **Strengthened detention capacity**: Expanded grounds for remanding 15-17 year olds in custody for repeat offenses, particularly gang-related crimes
2. **Increased sentence lengths**: For youth aged 15-17, proposed changes would increase maximum youth detention (sluten ungdomsvård, SUV) periods
3. **Enhanced follow-up regime**: Mandatory structured supervision for 12-24 months post-release, with clear escalation for violations
4. **Gang crime enhancement**: New provisions specifically targeting youth participation in criminal gang networks (similar to HD03218's adult provisions)

## Why It Matters

This proposition addresses Sweden's most acute domestic policy crisis. Since 2021, Sweden has had the highest per-capita rate of gun deaths in the EU — driven primarily by gang-related violence involving young men aged 15-25. The crime wave has been the single most dominant issue in Swedish politics, contributing significantly to the 2022 election result that brought Tidö coalition to power.

HD03246 represents year four of the Kristersson government's systematic "konsekvenstänkande" (consequences-based) approach: HD03218 (double sentences for gang crime), HD03217 (civil servant accountability), HD03235 (stricter deportation) and now HD03246 (youth offenders) form a coherent legislative architecture that the government will present as its crime-fighting legacy.

## Critical Assessment

**Politically strong, evidentially contested**: The proposition will pass because SD+M+KD have a majority and this is a core coalition demand. However, criminological evidence suggests that increased punitiveness for youth rarely achieves the stated goals of crime reduction. The government's case relies heavily on deterrence theory (clear consequences discourage crime) while the academic consensus emphasizes social causation (poverty, segregation, exposure to gang culture).

**Implementation gap**: SiS (youth detention facilities) is already operating near maximum capacity with staff shortages and internal violence problems. HD03246 will increase placement demand without a clear commitment to SiS resourcing.
