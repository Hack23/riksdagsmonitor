# Per-Document Analysis: HD03232

**Title**: Sveriges tillträde till konventionen om inrättande av en internationell skadeståndskommission för Ukraina  
**Prop. Number**: 2025/26:232  
**Minister**: Maria Malmer Stenergard (M, Utrikesdepartementet)  
**Committee**: Utrikesutskottet (UU)  
**Date**: 2026-04-16  
**Significance**: 8/10 🟩 HIGH

## Policy Summary

Sweden accedes to the convention establishing the International Reparations Commission for Ukraine — a multilateral body that will assess and register claims for reparations from Russia to Ukrainian victims of the war. This commission operates separately from the aggression tribunal (HD03231) — one prosecutes individuals, the other processes state-level reparations.

## Why It Matters

The reparations commission reflects a growing international consensus that Russia must ultimately pay for the destruction it has caused in Ukraine — estimated at over $500 billion in physical damage alone. The commission creates a legal registry of claims that will be used in future negotiations with Russia, or to distribute any seized Russian sovereign assets (approximately €300 billion currently frozen in Belgium via Euroclear).

Sweden's accession strengthens the commission's legitimacy and the global coalition demanding accountability.

## Connection to HD03231

HD03231 and HD03232 are designed as complementary instruments: the tribunal prosecutes individuals, the commission handles reparations. Both were developed under the Council of Europe's Register of Damage initiative, which Sweden has supported since its establishment in 2023.

## Financial Implications

The reparations commission membership creates Sweden's proportionate contribution to the commission's operating costs (estimated: ~10-15 MSEK annually). The larger question — whether frozen Russian sovereign assets can be legally transferred as reparations — remains unresolved in international law. Swedish Riksbanken's position on this will become relevant.
