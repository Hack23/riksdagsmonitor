# Per-Document Analysis: HD03231

**Title**: Sveriges anslutning till den utvidgade partiella överenskommelsen för den särskilda tribunalen för aggressionsbrottet mot Ukraina  
**Prop. Number**: 2025/26:231  
**Minister**: Maria Malmer Stenergard (M, Utrikesdepartementet)  
**Committee**: Utrikesutskottet (UU)  
**Date**: 2026-04-16  
**Significance**: 8/10 🟩 HIGH

## Policy Summary

Sweden joins the "expanded partial agreement" (Enlarged Partial Agreement, EPA) of the Council of Europe to establish a Special Tribunal for the Crime of Aggression against Ukraine. This tribunal, based in The Hague, will prosecute Russia's senior political and military leadership for the decision to invade Ukraine — a crime not within the existing jurisdiction of the International Criminal Court.

## Why It Matters

The Special Tribunal for Aggression represents a significant gap-filler in international criminal law. The ICC can prosecute war crimes and crimes against humanity committed in Ukraine, but not the crime of aggression itself (Russia is not an ICC member and the Kampala amendments don't apply). The new tribunal would specifically target Putin, Lavrov, and other architects of the illegal invasion.

Sweden joining as one of the founding members positions Stockholm as a constructive multilateral actor — critical for Sweden's post-NATO identity and efforts to build political weight within EU foreign policy.

## Historical Precedent

This is the most significant international criminal law development since the establishment of the ad hoc tribunals for Yugoslavia (ICTY) and Rwanda (ICTR) in the 1990s. Sweden helped establish both those tribunals and has a strong legacy in international humanitarian law (Dag Hammarskjöld, Folke Bernadotte, Raoul Wallenberg).
