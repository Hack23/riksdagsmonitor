## 🔬 Document Content Summary

Markus Kallifatides (S) challenges Infrastructure and Housing Minister Andreas Carlson (KD) over the government's failure to advance municipal pre-emption rights (förköpsrätt) for key properties. The interpellation traces the policy timeline:

- **June 2022**: Previous S government commissioned an investigation (dir. 2022:48) to propose broad municipal pre-emption rights — covering segregation, climate adaptation, large business establishments
- **May 2023**: Tidö government issued supplementary directives (dir. 2023:67), narrowing scope to only civil defence and organised crime prevention, removing anti-segregation grounds
- **May 2024**: Investigation delivered SOU 2024:38 "Digitala fastighetsköp & Förköpsrätt vid fastighetstransaktioner"
- **Status 2026**: SOU 2024:38 under review in Regeringskansliet — no proposition in the civilutskott's agenda for 2025/26

**Context**: Stockholm suburbs Sätra, Vårberg, Rågsved (all vulnerable areas with high crime/segregation) have been subject to property acquisitions by actors that municipal leaders wanted to block.

**Questions posed**:
1. What considerations led the minister and government not to progress the pre-emption proposals?
2. Does the minister still see no reason to introduce broader municipal pre-emption rights per the original directives?

## 📊 Political Intelligence Assessment

### Policy Background
Municipal pre-emption rights are a contested housing policy instrument. The original S-government directive would have given municipalities a powerful tool against segregation and speculative property investment. The Tidö government's narrowing to security/crime purposes reflects its ideological priorities (anti-segregation tools as market interference vs. security as legitimate state interest).
