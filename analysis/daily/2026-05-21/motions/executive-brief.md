## 60-second read (8 bullets)

1. **S vs. MP on household debt (2026-05-20):** S demands full rejection of prop 255 (GDPR/privacy framing); MP accepts the mechanism but wants corporate debt + Riksbank added — contrasting positions on the same bill from within the same opposition bloc.
2. **Prop 255 substance:** Government wants authority to sample household debt data directly from banks/credit institutions via SCB. Sweden's household debt is ~89-91% of GDP (IMF WEO-2026-04) — one of the EU's highest. S argues the monitoring mechanism is disproportionate; MP argues it's insufficient for macro-prudential purposes.
3. **C constitutional challenge (prop 258, KU):** C (HD024184) accepts party-finance transparency and lobbying registration but demands rejection of the labor-org contributions section — citing Lagrådet's "bräckligt" (fragile) opinion, SOU 2025:52 committee dissent, and ECHR Art.11 freedom of association risk.
4. **Migration (8 motions, SfU):** V and MP oppose all four migration propositions (262-265). S joins on props 262, 263, 264 but did NOT file on prop 265 (family reunification) — indicating S is still more cautious than V+MP on family immigration.
5. **Defense (FöU):** V demands full rejection of prop 254 (Finland-Norway military cooperation); MP accepts cooperation in principle but seeks Swedish sovereignty safeguards.
6. **Transport (TU):** V and MP both oppose Skr 259 (national transport plan 2026-2037), demanding greater rail and climate investment vs. government's road-dominant approach.
7. **Health (SoU):** V and MP oppose prop 251 (substance abuse/psychiatric care reform), arguing insufficient structural integration of psychiatry and addiction treatment.
8. **Electoral calculus:** Government + SD = 176-178; majority threshold = 175. All propositions pass. Opposition motions serve as election-year position papers, campaign differentiation, and ECHR/ECJ evidence records.
