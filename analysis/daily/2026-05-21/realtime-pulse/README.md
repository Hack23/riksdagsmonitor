## Summary

Sweden's Riksdag today debates and schedules plenary votes on **four significant committee reports** led by JuU28 — the landmark betänkande on Police use of AI for real-time facial recognition. The vote on biometric surveillance marks a constitutional inflection point: Sweden is adopting technology capable of systematic public-space surveillance just 115 days before a general election, with only a weak "exceptional circumstances" safeguard and a three-year sunset clause. Simultaneously, new civil legislation (CU36 area cooperation fee, CU41 hydropower habitat exemptions) and capital-market reform (FiU40 fund market) advance the Tidö coalition's pre-election legislative sprint. Three interpellations — on southern Swedish water scarcity, Köping hospital closure threats, and constitutional reform — and seven written questions (covering Taiwan arms sales, Tibet/China dialogue, pension trust, border controls) round out a day dominated by security, democratic accountability, and welfare service themes.
