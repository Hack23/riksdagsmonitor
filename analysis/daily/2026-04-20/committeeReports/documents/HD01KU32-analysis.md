## 2. Core Analysis

### What the Proposal Does
KU32 proposes amendments to both the Freedom of the Press Act (TF) and the Freedom of Expression Act (YGL) to allow ordinary legislation to impose accessibility requirements on media products and services that are constitutionally protected. 

Currently, because TF and YGL grant media extensive autonomy from ordinary legislation, digital accessibility requirements (e.g., subtitles, audio descriptions, alternative text) CANNOT be mandated for newspapers, radio, TV, and online media through normal laws. This creates a paradox: Sweden cannot fully implement the EU's **European Accessibility Act** (EAA) for media sectors.

This amendment is also adopted "vilande" — requiring second reading after the 2026 election.

### Policy Significance
This is a **EU compliance fix** more than a politically divisive measure. The European Accessibility Act requires accessible digital products across the EU from June 2025. Sweden's constitutional framework prevents compliance for media sectors without this TF/YGL amendment.

**Disability rights dimension**: Sweden has ~500,000 persons with significant visual/hearing impairments who benefit from accessible media. The amendment enables legal requirements for subtitles, screen readers, and audio descriptions in TF/YGL-protected media.
