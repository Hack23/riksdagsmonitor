## Executive Summary

Prime Minister Ulf Kristersson's government (Moderaterna + Kristdemokraterna + Liberalerna, supported by Sverigedemokraterna on confidence and supply) has deployed its most comprehensive pre-election legislative package of the 2025/26 Riksdag session: **9 significant propositions tabled April 9–16, 2026**, less than five months before the September 13, 2026 general election.

The centrepiece is Finance Minister **Elisabeth Svantesson's 2026 Spring Economic Bill** (Prop. 2025/26:100, dok_id HD03100) — the fiscal policy framework document against which the entire 2026 election campaign will be argued. It arrives with Sweden's 2024 GDP growth at only **+0.82%**, materially below Denmark (+3.48%), Norway (+2.10%), and the euro-area average. Sweden contracted −0.20% in 2023; the Spring Bill must credibly project recovery to 2026 growth of ~1.8–2.2% to anchor the government's economic competence narrative.

The legislative package is strategically coherent across four policy clusters:

1. **Fiscal cluster** — HD03100 + HD0399 + HD03236 establish economic credibility and deliver visible cost-of-living relief.
2. **Energy cluster** — HD03240 + HD03239 + HD03238 position Sweden for green industrial investment.
3. **Justice cluster** — HD03237 + HD03246 fulfil SD coalition commitments on police expansion and youth-criminal-code tightening.
4. **Security cluster** — HD03220 + HD03231 + HD03232 reinforce Sweden's post-NATO-accession alliance credibility.

The extra amendment budget (HD03236), cutting fuel taxes by SEK 0.50–0.80/litre and providing el-/gas-price support, is the most overtly electoral measure. It targets **Segment 1 rural drivers (~1.2 million voters)** and **Segment 2 suburban homeowners (~1.8 million)** with visible relief timed for pump-price effect by early July 2026.

Our integrated analysis (historical-parallels + scenario-analysis + voter-segmentation + devils-advocate) converges on a **retention probability of 35–40%** for the Kristersson coalition — meaningfully improved by the package but constrained by Sweden's GDP gap vs. Nordic peers and persistent polling baseline of roughly 47% vs. 51% for the opposition bloc.

## Key Findings

### Finding 1: Pre-Election Fiscal Offensive — Significance 47/50

The simultaneous submission of three budget propositions (HD03100, HD0399, HD03236) within 72 hours represents a coordinated electoral strategy. The Spring Economic Bill establishes the macro narrative; the Spring Amendment Budget implements technical adjustments; the Extra Amendment Budget delivers popular relief. Together, Svantesson and Financial Markets Minister Niklas Wykman (KD) construct a "responsible government that delivers" campaign argument.

**Historical parallel** (`historical-parallels.md`): Comparable pre-election triplets under Reinfeldt 2010 (retained), Reinfeldt 2014 (lost), Andersson 2022 (lost). GDP tailwind is the dominant predictor; Sweden's current +0.82% places Kristersson in the historically hostile "< +1.5%" cohort.

**Critical context**: Riksrevisionen has issued its fiscal-framework report (HD03241) alongside; any adverse follow-up finding would directly damage HD03100 credibility.

### Finding 2: Energy Transition Infrastructure — Significance 35/50

Ministers Lotta Edholm and Johan Britz at Klimat- och näringslivsdepartementet have produced a logically integrated energy reform package:
- HD03240 (Nya lagar om elsystemet) creates the electricity market framework for industrial electrification.
- HD03239 (Vindkraft i kommuner) solves the NIMBY problem via municipal revenue-sharing — a model pioneered by Denmark in 2011 (`international-comparative.md`).
- HD03238 (Ny myndighet för miljöprövning) aims to cut environmental permit timelines from 24–36 months to claimed 6–12 months; realistic independent estimate 15–20 months by 2028 (`implementation-feasibility.md`).

This positions Sweden to capitalise on European green industrial investment. Estimated **SEK 80–120 bn green industrial investment** could be unlocked 2027–2030 under best-case execution (`scenario-analysis.md` Cluster B1).

### Finding 3: Police Expansion Structural Reform — Significance 34/50

Justice Minister **Gunnar Strömmer's paid police training** (HD03237) removes the financial barrier that has deterred qualified applicants. Sweden is the **last Nordic country** with unpaid police training — HD03237 is a catch-up policy, not a pioneering one (`international-comparative.md`).

Swedish police force ~21,500 officers vs. target 27,000 (by 2030). Implementation risk is moderate-to-high: Polismyndigheten capacity to absorb 2–3x application surge requires facility expansion and 40–60 additional instructors (`implementation-feasibility.md`).

Red-team concern (`devils-advocate.md`): paid training could produce **quality-selection pressure** if application volume triples and selection standards loosen. Probability P(0.25) of measurable quality degradation 2027–2029.

### Finding 4: NATO Defence Credibility — Significance 38/50

HD03220 — Swedish contribution to NATO Enhanced Forward Presence in Finland. Foreign Minister **Maria Malmer Stenergard** and Minister for Foreign Trade **Benjamin Dousa** signed alongside PM Kristersson. The deployment is battalion-level, Q4 2026 rotation, with full consensus across government + S + C; only V maintains traditional defence scepticism.

Combined with HD03231 (Ukraine aggression tribunal) and HD03232 (Ukraine compensation commission), the Kristersson government is constructing a **coherent "Sweden as active security contributor" narrative** that contrasts with the pre-2022 neutrality tradition.

### Finding 5: Digital Consumer Protection — Significance 27/50

HD03233 (anti-fraud electronic communications) mandates carrier obligations to block number spoofing and verify caller ID. Estimated Swedish scam losses SEK 500+ million annually, hitting pensioners disproportionately. Enjoys universal cross-party support.

Historical context: Finland's Traficom implemented equivalent rules in 2022; Sweden is again following Nordic peers (`international-comparative.md`).

### Finding 6: Environmental Permitting Agency — Significance 30/50

HD03238 creates a dedicated national environmental-review agency. The most **distinctively ambitious** reform in the package by European comparison — Denmark achieved similar results through coordination rather than new-agency creation; Germany used legal "overriding interest" provisions (`international-comparative.md`).

Implementation risk HIGH: new-agency standup in Sweden has consistently taken 24+ months (Myndigheten för digital förvaltning precedent). Realistic measurable impact on permit timelines: 2028–2029, not 2027 as government narrative implies (`implementation-feasibility.md`).

### Finding 7: Youth Criminal Code & Women's Safety (Adjacent)

HD03246 (stricter rules for young offenders) and Skr. 2025/26:245 (national strategy against men's violence against women) complement the justice and welfare agenda. HD03246 addresses Sweden's gang-recruitment crisis (under-21 perpetrators in ~60% of shootings 2024); Skr. 245 implements a 10-year strategy aligned with Istanbul Convention obligations.

### Finding 8: Ukraine Accountability (HD03231, HD03232)

Sweden accedes to the Council of Europe Special Tribunal for the Crime of Aggression and the International Compensation Commission for Ukraine. These are **constitutionally non-controversial** — but they embed Sweden in post-war European legal-institutional architecture in ways that will outlast any single government.

### Finding 9: Rural / Industrial Policy Complement (HD03242, HD03243, HD03244)

Rural Minister Peter Kullgren's HD03242 clarifies forestry regulation; Svantesson's HD03243 reforms shipping tonnage tax; Civil Affairs Minister Erik Slottner's HD03244 mandates public-sector data interoperability. These are **industrial-policy legacy** measures that support the green-transition thesis and reduce regulatory friction for 290 municipalities and 21 regions.
