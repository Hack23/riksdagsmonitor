## Detailed Comparison — Fiscal Stimulus

### Denmark 2024–2026: The "Frederiksen Discipline" Model

Mette Frederiksen's SR+V+M government has maintained fiscal surpluses while implementing targeted middle-income tax cuts. Core features:
- GDP +3.48% (2024) — aided by pharma (Novo Nordisk/Ozempic effect, ~1.5 pp of GDP).
- Active labour market spending + digitalisation rather than consumer relief.
- DKK 15 bn income tax cuts implemented 2024–2025 for households at 300k–600k DKK.
- Energy: consumers paid full market price; government provided one-time DKK 6,000 per household in 2022, phased out by 2024.

**Sweden 2026 contrast**: HD03236 extends the "bridge relief" beyond Denmark's cut-off, reflecting Sweden's weaker recovery.

### Norway 2024–2026: The "Oil-Fund Cushion" Model

Jonas Gahr Støre's Arbeiderparti-led minority has used sovereign wealth fund (Pensjonsfond Utland) capacity for pre-election support:
- GDP +2.10% (2024) — sustained by oil revenue cycle + mainland productivity.
- NOK 30 bn electricity price support through 2024 (strømstøtte).
- Regulated electricity pricing structurally; Norway retains near-universal household price support.

**Sweden 2026 contrast**: Sweden has no oil-fund; every SEK of relief comes from current fiscal space. HD03236 is therefore smaller in proportion and more fiscally constrained than Norway's parallel measures.

### Finland 2024–2026: The "Orpo Austerity" Model

Petteri Orpo's centre-right coalition (Kok + PS + KD + RKP) has pursued aggressive fiscal consolidation:
- GDP +0.42% (2024) — barely recovering from 2023 recession.
- No pre-election consumer relief; focus on wage moderation and labour market reform.
- EUR 6 bn expenditure cuts programme 2023–2027.

**Sweden 2026 contrast**: Sweden's coalition has space to deliver HD03236 relief that Finland could not politically or fiscally afford. This is Sweden's structural advantage in its Nordic comparison.

### Germany 2024–2026: The "Schuldenbremse Unlock" Model

Friedrich Merz's CDU/CSU–SPD coalition (from May 2025) unlocked EUR 500 bn infrastructure fund via constitutional amendment. Core features:
- Ambitious defence + infrastructure investment.
- No direct consumer relief comparable to HD03236.

**Sweden 2026 contrast**: Germany's model exchanges short-term relief for long-term investment. Sweden's HD03236 is the opposite — short-term relief without matching investment scale (investment is in the energy cluster, not household relief).

## Environmental Permitting Reform Comparative

### Denmark's Erhvervsministerium Model (2023)
- Existing Miljøstyrelsen + Erhvervsstyrelsen + regional authorities coordinated reform.
- Single digital entry point; median permit timeline cut from 24 to 10 months.
- Did **not** create new standalone agency — instead rationalised existing ones.
- Key factor: small country, dense inter-agency coordination.

### Germany's Beschleunigungsgesetz (2023)
- Federal acceleration law for renewable project permits.
- Granted renewable energy "overriding public interest" status — reducing legal challenge surface.
- Did not create new agency.
- Result: ~30–40% timeline reduction within 12 months.

### Finland's Integrated Environment-Climate Agency (2023)
- Merged environmental and climate policy functions.
- Appeared similar to Swedish HD03238 but built ON TOP of existing structures.

### Netherlands' "Nitrogen Crisis" approach (2019–2024)
- Courts forced agricultural and construction permit system redesign.
- Shows what happens when permitting fails: years of litigation, agricultural protests, construction freeze.

**Key insight for HD03238**: Sweden's new-agency approach is **more ambitious** than Denmark (coordination) or Germany (legal acceleration). But it is also **riskier**: creating new agencies takes 3–5 years to reach full effectiveness. Denmark's coordination approach delivered faster results.

**Policy recommendation implied by comparison**: Complement HD03238 with digital-single-entry-point measures from Denmark and legal "overriding interest" provisions from Germany.
