# Stakeholder Perspectives — Opposition Motions 2026-05-11

## Primary Stakeholders

### Governing Bloc (M+KD+L with SD support)
**Interests**: Pass both propositions before summer recess; maintain electoral narrative on law and order; avoid forestry EU entanglement.
**Position**: Proceed unamended. Government legal memo provides sufficient rebuttal to Lagrådet's RF concerns. Forestry bill is good for owners and the economy.
**Confidence**: Comfortable parliamentary majority on both propositions absent a C reversal.
**Vulnerability**: The "ignoring the constitution" narrative from C; EU Habitats risk.

### Centerpartiet
**Interests**: Differentiate electorally; maintain "responsible liberal" brand; secure better forestry policy and RF-compliant youth crime policy.
**Position on skogsbruk (HD024145)**: The proposition is a step in the right direction but insufficient. SOU 2025:93 production incentives must be implemented. C will likely vote "yes" on passage but with caveats.
**Position on JuU (HD024146)**: Cannot support age-cut as written — unconstitutional per Lagrådet. C will vote against the age-cut provision. Whether C proposes a formal amendment or merely votes against remains unclear.
**Electoral signalling**: C needs to occupy the "constitutionally serious" space vacated by KD's rightward shift. Both motions serve this purpose.

### Socialdemokraterna
**Interests**: Position as the responsible, evidence-based alternative; delay forestry bill if possible; avoid appearing soft on crime.
**Position on skogsbruk (HD024144)**: Demand cumulative impact analysis — this is procedurally legitimate and delays are electorally neutral for S.
**Position on JuU**: Silent in committee motions; likely floor vote against. Tactical silence preserves S's flexibility on what alternative they'd offer.
**Electoral calculation**: Urban voters care about biodiversity; suburban voters care about crime. S must thread both.

### Sverigedemokraterna
**Interests**: Maintain supply-and-confidence; signal responsible governance modifier on forestry; avoid being seen as blindly supporting failed policy.
**Position on skogsbruk (HD024143)**: Supportive but demands small-owner exemption (<50 ha). This is a genuine constituency concern for SD's rural landowner voters.
**Position on JuU**: No motion filed; no known opposition. SD's core constituency strongly supports tough youth crime measures.
**Strategic note**: SD's moderation on forestry (technical qualification) while backing youth crime toughening reflects their electoral coalition.

### Vänsterpartiet
**Interests**: Full rejection, environmental protection, constitutional defence, social justice on criminal law.
**HD024141 perspective**: Biodiversity crisis framing; V sees the forestry bill as a catastrophic step toward irreversible habitat loss. Skogsstyrelsen 2026:07 data (26,000 ha/year) is central to V's evidence base.
**HD024142 perspective**: Lagrådet, brain development research, risk of criminalising children with social vulnerabilities. V's motion cites scientific consensus on adolescent brain development to argue 13-year-olds should not face criminal proceedings.
**Leverage**: Very limited — V is in opposition, can delay media narrative but cannot block passage.

### Miljöpartiet
**Interests**: Environmental protection; EU nature law compliance; restorative justice.
**HD024147**: Full rejection of forestry bill, emphasising 2030 biodiversity targets and EU Habitats compatibility.
**HD024148**: Opposes age cut and specific reduced youth discount provisions; MP frames through UN children's rights lens.
**Leverage**: Limited; MP is not a supply-and-confidence actor. MP's EU political connections (Greens/EFA group) could amplify EU law risk.

### Lagrådet
**Interests**: Constitutional rule of law; Lagrådet's advisory authority.
**Position**: Yttrande 12 March 2026 stated RF 2 kap. 8, 20–21 §§ incompatibility with age-cut provision.
**Significance**: Lagrådet is the only actor in this cluster whose position is legally binding in the sense that it creates political-constitutional accountability. If the government overrides Lagrådet, it must justify this explicitly in the legislative record — the justification then becomes the basis for any future court challenge.

### Skogsstyrelsen
**Interests**: Regulatory mandate execution; data integrity.
**Role**: Skogsstyrelsen 2026:07 (cited by V in HD024141) provides the empirical foundation for the biodiversity risk argument. The agency is executing the existing supervisory mandate which the government is now proposing to weaken.
**Tension**: Skogsstyrelsen as a government agency cannot publicly oppose a government proposition, but its data publication is a form of institutional constraint.

### EU Commission / DG Environment
**Interests**: Habitats Directive Art. 6 compliance; Nature Restoration Law implementation.
**Status**: No formal action yet. The Art. 6 compatibility risk noted in HD024141 would require a formal notification or complaint to trigger Commission interest.
**Potential role**: Could emerge as a slow-moving but high-impact player if prop. 242 passes and NGOs file a formal EU complaint.
