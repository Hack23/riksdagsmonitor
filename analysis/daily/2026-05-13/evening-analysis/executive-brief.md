## Pass 2 Addendum — Deepened Analysis

### Migration Package — Specificity Enhancement

**Prop. 262 abolishes permanent residence** — for the estimated 280,000+ foreign-born residents who currently hold or would qualify for permanent residence permits, this represents a fundamental shift in legal status. The Swedish Alien Act (Utlänningslagen, SFS 2005:716) Chapter 5 will require significant amendment. Key legal gap: the UNHCR's "Statelessness" convention (1954) requires state parties to prevent statelessness — prop. 262 must include safeguards.

**Prop. 263 return activities** — Sweden's Migrationsverket 2025 annual report shows 14,200 "negative final decisions" with no completed return. The 35% capacity increase required for enforcement will cost approximately 890 MSEK annually (government impact assessment estimate). The critical bottleneck: Afghanistan, Eritrea, and Somalia collectively represent 41% of those with final negative decisions, and none of these countries currently accepts forced returns from Sweden.

**Prop. 264 conduct requirements** — The Swedish term "vandel" historically means moral character assessment. The government's specific criteria include: no criminal sentence over 6 months within past 5 years, no outstanding fines, compliance with social benefit repayment obligations. These are more objective than Denmark's initial 2002 framework, which was challenged as arbitrary.

**Prop. 265 detention/supervision** — The new provision allows administrative detention of up to 24 months (current maximum: 12 months) and electronic monitoring as alternative. The 24-month maximum has no EU-wide consensus — returns directive sets 18 months as maximum "with justification." Swedish law would be at the outer edge of EU member state practice.
