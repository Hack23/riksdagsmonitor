## BLUF (Bottom Line Up Front)

The Riksdag's Finance (FiU) and Justice (JuU) committees have advanced eight legislative items this week. **Three deserve immediate attention**: (1) a new financial-sector crisis management function under Riksbanken's leadership that reshapes Sweden's systemic risk architecture, (2) a landmark psychological-violence criminal statute that creates a new Category-A criminal offense, and (3) tighter police powers at public demonstrations with constitutional implications that Lagrådet should formally examine.

All three take effect **1 July 2026** — six weeks before Sweden's general election campaign enters its final sprint. Legislative calendar alignment with the election cycle is unlikely to be coincidental.

## Priority Intelligence Items

### P1 — HD01FiU37: Financial Sector Crisis Management (L3)
**Subject**: Riksbanken assumes coordination leadership for operational crisis management in the Swedish financial sector. Finansinspektionen (FI) and Riksgäldskontoret participate.  
**Significance**: Sweden operationalises the EU's DORA resilience framework at a systemic level. Creates a new permanent inter-agency body with Riksbanken as primus motor. No significant opposition — unanimous committee position.  
**Risk**: Concentration of crisis authority in Riksbanken raises governance questions around accountability (Riksbanken not answerable to minister in normal channels). First test under actual stress will reveal institutional capacity gaps.  
**Election angle**: Pro-stability narrative, no voter controversy; useful to government as evidence of fiscal/financial prudence.

### P2 — HD01JuU39: Psychological Violence as Criminal Offense (L2+)
**Subject**: New crime of "psychological violence" (psykiskt våld) introduced in brottsbalken, max 4 years imprisonment. Covers repeated humiliation, threats, coercion, surveillance causing serious self-harm risk.  
**Significance**: Broadens domestic violence law significantly. Consistent with Nordic trend (Norway 2005, Denmark 2012). Four-party opposition (S, V, C, MP) reservations on three sub-areas: economic violence, conversion attempts, training requirements.  
**Risk**: Implementation burden on police/prosecutors to prove "repeated pattern + psychological damage" threshold — enforcement gap likely for 2–3 years.  
**Election angle**: Framing contest: government claims protecting women; opposition claims law is too narrow (missing economic coercion and conversion therapy).

### P3 — HD01JuU32: Police Powers at Public Gatherings (L2+)
**Subject**: JuU rejects most opposition motions on police powers at demonstrations. Existing ordningslagen + polislagen provisions maintained or tightened.  
**Significance**: Constitutional tension with RF chapter 2 §1 (freedom of assembly). Lagrådet did not formally review all changes — gap to monitor.  
**Risk**: Risk of EU/ECHR challenge (Art. 11 ECHR — freedom of peaceful assembly) if powers applied disproportionately in election year.  
**Election angle**: High salience: SD and KD call for stronger police powers; S and MP defend assembly rights. Expect campaign use.

## Pass 2 Additions — Specific Evidence

### FiU37 Institutional Detail
The new crisis coordination function under Riksbanken is specifically motivated by Sweden's experience in 2015–2020 (Swedbank anti-money laundering failures + Baltic banking exposure) and the COVID-19 period where operational resilience gaps in Swedish payment infrastructure became apparent. Riksbanken's RIX payment system had near-misses in Q2 2020. FiU37 creates the statutory basis for Riksbanken to act as coordinator — it did NOT have this authority before.

### JuU39 Reservation Map — Specific Detail
- **Reservation 1 (S+MP)**: Demands economic violence be explicit sub-category. This mirrors Norway's § 282 which explicitly includes "severe economic deprivation." UK Serious Crime Act s.76 implicitly includes financial control. Sweden's JuU39 is narrower.
- **Reservation 2 (S+V+C+MP)**: 4-party, signals that all centre-to-left parties agree on conversion therapy prohibition. This is the most politically explosive reservation as it directly contradicts some KD/SD social conservative positions.
- **Reservations 3+4 (S, MP separately)**: Both demand Brå study mandate. Government's refusal to bind implementation with statutory Brå review is notable — suggests confidence or political reluctance to create a post-election accountability mechanism.

### JuU32 Constitutional Mechanism
RF chapter 2 §20 allows restrictions on freedom of assembly only through law (lag), and §21 requires the restriction to be "necessary in a democratic society." The proportionality test in §21 is Sweden's domestication of ECHR Art. 11 §2 language. Specific JuU32 provisions on pre-screening and risk-based surveillance need to pass this two-part test. The concern is not that restrictions are impossible — it is that without Lagrådet's proportionality analysis, the drafting quality is uncertain.

### P4 — HD01UbU28: Teacher Credentialing for 10-Year Primary School (L2)
**Subject**: UbU (Education Committee) unanimously approves administrative adjustment of teacher legitimation credentials ahead of 2028/29 introduction of the tioårig grundskola.  
**Significance**: Closes the last statutory gap in the Tidö government's flagship education reform. Unanimous cross-party adoption (no reservations from S, V, C, MP) signals operational consensus even where political opposition existed at the policy design phase.  
**Election angle**: Medium. Government can claim "completed the 10-year school reform" as full legislative package. Unanimous adoption prevents opposition from attacking this element.  
**Effective**: 2028/29 school year — post-election, but legislative completion pre-election.
