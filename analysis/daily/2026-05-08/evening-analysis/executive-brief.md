## Pass 2 Improvements — Specific Evidence and Analytical Sharpening

### CU31 — Enhanced Evidence

Sweden has approximately **1.8 million rent-controlled hyresrätt apartments** (Statistikmyndigheten SCB, Bostads- och byggnadstistik 2025). Of these, an estimated 600,000 are in Stockholm municipality alone. The CU31 reform applies market pricing to **new-build** properties only — the existing stock remains under bruksvärde. However, the reform creates a dual-track market where: (a) new-build market-rate units compete with (b) heavily subsidised existing stock, incentivising landlords to demolish and rebuild rather than renovate existing rent-controlled properties. This is the **gentrification-by-demolition risk** that Hyresgästföreningen will centre its legal challenge on.

**Quantitative benchmark**: Finland's 1995 rent reform liberalised all private rentals. Within 5 years: new-build construction increased 18%; average market rents in Helsinki increased 32%; waiting lists for social housing increased 45%. Sweden's reform is more limited (new-build only), but the directional risk is established by Nordic comparison.

### HD11802 — Mohamsson Context

Simona Mohamsson became L's Integration and Education Minister in 2022 — the first minister of Muslim background in Swedish government history. Her political identity as L's "integration success story" makes any concession to SD's veil ban position not merely politically inconvenient but existentially threatening to L's brand. Previous Swedish governments (Fredrik Reinfeldt's 2006-2014 M government) explicitly rejected full veil bans as incompatible with Swedish constitutional freedoms. The government already has **school dress code provisions** (Skollag 2010:800, §6) that allow schools to restrict face coverings where they impede education — this is the response Mohamsson will cite.

### HD11803 — Flotilla Legal Context  

The vessel interception occurred in **international waters** (Gazaremsan/internationellt hav). Under **UNCLOS Part VII** (High Seas) and customary international law, interdiction of a third-country flagged vessel requires either: (a) the vessel state's consent, (b) UN Security Council authorization, or (c) a recognised exception (piracy, slave trade, etc.). None of these conditions applies to a civilian humanitarian flotilla. The S question (Büser) is legally well-grounded — making the minister's response more constrained than on purely diplomatic issues where law is ambiguous.
